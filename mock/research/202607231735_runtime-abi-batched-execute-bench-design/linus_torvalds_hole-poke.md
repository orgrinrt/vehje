# Does this bench earn its keep (Linus Torvalds)

## Verdict in one line

The infrastructure is genuinely good and the negative results are worth keeping, but the headline conclusion is a tautology measured against a payload constructed to produce it, and the regime the design itself declared decisive (cheap payload, native tier) was never built into the boundary families, so the arc has so far confirmed the obvious and not yet measured the question.

## Where it is a strawman or the conclusion is trivial/over-claimed (numbered, concrete)

1. **The W < 8 rows of the headline table compare scalar against scalar and call it "no win."** `carrier-runtime/src/lib.rs:121`: `soa_batch` runs `while i + LANES <= w` and then a scalar remainder. At W = 1, 2, 4 the SoA entry executes the scalar interpreter, period. `first-look-results.md` rows W=1..4 showing "~1.0x (no win)" are an identity, not a measurement. The "threshold at W >= 8" is the hardcoded `const LANES: usize = 8` at `lib.rs:43` reflected back as a discovery. "The design premise, now measured" (first-look point 2) is circular: the bench discovered its own constant.

2. **"Expose a batched entry taking W >= the SIMD lane width" is a restatement of "an 8-lane vector needs 8 lanes."** That is arithmetic, not an ABI finding. The genuine ABI questions (does the crossing cost erode the win; does entry form matter; does the sink shape matter) all got the same answer in the first look, "invisible under this payload," which was computable in advance: a resolved-pointer `blr` costs ~10 ns and one 256-node interpret costs ~8.5 us per record. A 500:1 ratio was never going to produce an interesting crossing number. Nothing swept in `abi_cross_scalar_real` or `abi_soa_win_real` could have moved the decision.

3. **The decisive regime is not in the built machinery.** The synthesis's own headline framing (`synthesis_and_build-plan.md`, "The headline is a ratio" section) says the batched-ABI win scales with `C_cross / I_payload` and is largest at the native tier, "the case a scalar-per-record ABI most penalises." The floor ladder rung (d) claims "native ceiling, crossed." But `carrier/src/bench/boundary/cross.rs:30` says outright: "Rungs d (native ceiling) and e (null-dispatch) live in their own families," i.e. the pre-existing IN-PROCESS carrier families. Grep of `boundary/*.rs` finds no native-payload cell crossing the cdylib. Every boundary family pins `PROG_NODES = 256` (`common.rs:27`); the six profiles vary op mix, not payload magnitude. So all 10 families operate exclusively in the regime where the crossing is provably invisible, and the regime where entry form, sink shape, and C_cross actually discriminate does not exist in the tree. The three expert reviews of the built machinery did not flag this. The "multi-hour full run" currently in progress will not fix it; it runs the same families.

4. **The "record" is degenerate relative to the settled contracts of the same round.** One u64 seed in, one u64 checksum out. The round's own output topic (`202607201315_topic.runtime-output-value-transfer.md:12,40`) says the real output is "records within records within records, arbitrarily large trees" crossing as a region-structured value-arena through reserve/commit. The sink bench (`carrier-runtime/src/lib.rs:382-423`) writes exactly one u64 per record into the arena. It measures the reverse-crossing count, fine, but it cannot see the actual ABI question the settled contract raises: whether per-record value-tree serialisation batches (shared region per batch vs per record), which is where an output-side batching decision would live. Similarly the payload has zero runtime-environment interaction, while the framework's identity is effects and handlers; a per-record effect is a serial component that Amdahls the 2.4x down, and no family models it.

5. **The marshalling family measures a strawman access pattern.** `cr_execute_marshal_aos/soa` (`lib.rs:437-496`) XOR-folds a record's f fields into one seed BEFORE evaluation. No real interpreter does that: the program reads specific fields during evaluation via field-access ops (the real AoS/SoA question is gather vs contiguous lane loads inside the vectorised interpret, interacting with the SoA win). What was built is "which direction do you stride a buffer while feeding a hash," which is a cache question answerable from first principles and unrelated to the marshalling contract of the ABI.

6. **The masked-SoA cost, named by the arc's own settled decision A as a bench consequence, was not built.** `synthesis_and_build-plan.md` decision A closes with "the internal SoA mask-and-continue cost (all-success SoA vs masked SoA in the payload bench)." Grep for mask in `boundary/` and `vertical.rs`: no masked cell exists. The one payload variation that could shrink the headline 2.4x under realistic fault rates is absent.

7. **The two "genuine open decisions" were both settled without bench data.** Decision A by design reasoning, decision B (Wmax = 128) by op fiat. The data shows the win saturating at W = 8 and flat to 256; nothing measured distinguishes Wmax = 8 from 128. Fine as a fiat, but the arc should not imply the number is evidence-backed.

8. **The first look publishes "the first-look ABI answer" from the one regime its own model says is uninformative.** It caveats correctly at the end, but the bolded answer paragraph is stated as the ABI decision while resting on interpret-heavy payloads only. Under `strict-by-design` discipline that is a green declared before the decisive arm ran; the honest state is "confirmed the crossing is free under fat payloads; decision still pending the cheap-payload regime," which currently cannot run because of item 3.

## What actually has value here

- **The two-object discipline is right and reusable.** A same-crate loop under fat LTO genuinely cannot represent per-call count (`synthesis_and_build-plan.md`, "Why the two-object split"); the shared `carrier-runtime` cdylib with resolved pointers, and the upstream `boundary` regime, is real harness capital.
- **The correctness hygiene is exemplary.** Byte-exact cross-validation of every entry form against in-process replicas (`lib.rs:566-583`, `boundary/soa.rs:116`), the ICF distinct-address check on the 16 cold targets (`lib.rs:645-666`), the sink content check (`lib.rs:603`). This is how bench machinery should be built, no argument.
- **C_cross ~ 10-15 ns is a real, citable constant.** Bench 1's null_entry numbers permanently kill two future bad arguments: "FFI is expensive so we must batch" (it is not; batching is justified by vectorisation, not crossing amortisation, and the first look states this correctly at point 3) and "we must monomorphise per-W entry symbols for speed" (all entry forms will measure equal; ship the simplest symbol surface). Negative results that prevent ABI gold-plating are worth money.
- **The decision itself is correct.** A columnar eval engine behind a per-record FFI would be malpractice; the batched entry was always the answer. The arc's real function was to make that a recorded, evidence-shaped fact instead of an assertion, and to size the crossing so nobody relitigates it.

## What should have been measured instead (if anything)

1. **Sweep residual size, not just op mix.** `PROG_NODES` in {4, 16, 64, 256, 1024}. `C_cross / I_payload` is driven by program size at least as much as profile, and "thousands of tiny residuals over big columns" is the actual consumer workload shape (a game-trigger residual is tens of nodes, not 256). One added axis buys more decision-relevant information than the marshal, lifecycle, and residency families combined.
2. **Build the boundary-crossed native-ceiling payload FIRST.** It is the regime the design names decisive, it is the only place entry form and sink shape can discriminate, and it is the one family that does not exist. Until it runs, every "negligible" claim is scoped to interpret-tier payloads and should say so.
3. **A field-reading payload.** Records with f fields living in host columns, read by field-access ops during evaluation (gather vs contiguous per layout). That is the real marshalling/layout question; delete or demote the XOR-prefold family.
4. **Masked SoA at fault rates 0 / 1 / 10 percent.** Decision A's own named consequence. This is the number that says whether the 2.4x survives real scripts.
5. **A value-shaped output.** Even a fixed 3-node tree per record through the reserve/commit sink instead of one u64, so the output contract that was actually settled is the one being costed.

The machinery is a good chassis. Point it at the payloads the design says matter, and stop headline-quoting the lane width back at yourself.
