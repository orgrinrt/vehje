# Staging and the line: IR-to-IR expansion, and where compilation actually lives

**Author lens:** partial evaluation, multi-stage programming, LMS.
**Read:** the source (`vehje-lower`, `vehje-ir`, `vehje-codegen`, `vehje-typecheck`, `vehje-runtime-abi`), the two runtime deepdives, the topic, the bench findings.

## One-line reading

The IR-to-IR reframing is partial evaluation done with the correct vehicle (emit a fresh program, do not mutate the old one), and the "compile/runtime line" is not a line: it is the four-point binding-time lattice the IR already carries (`grade.rs:105`), where a compile stage is replicated at every binding time that is not the last, so literal const-inlining commits the framework to a specializer that must exist twice, once in Rust and once inside the Zig runtime, and the framework already names this debt but does not yet discharge the axis that governs it.

## What the current design gets right

The move from the redirecting `Rewrite` to an emit-into-a-fresh-arena pass is the right correction, and the reason is structural, not stylistic. `Rewrite::resolve` (`vehje-lower/src/lib.rs:55-68`) can only ever redirect a node to a strictly smaller arena index; the whole soundness of the side-table rests on that monotone-decrease invariant (`lib.rs:62-66`, restated in `DEEPDIVE_LOWERING.md.tmpl:33-39`). That makes `Rewrite` a *reduction-only* rewriter: it selects a subgraph of what already exists (fold `if true` to its taken branch at `lib.rs:149-159`; share a duplicate to its earlier occurrence at `lib.rs:288-307`). Macro expansion and A-normal-form hoisting are *productive* rewrites: they must create nodes that are not in the input. A productive rewrite cannot be expressed as a downward projection, which is exactly why `Anf` and `MacroExpand` are honest no-ops today (`lib.rs:387-393`, `400-408`). op's correction identifies the real fault: the constraint was never no-alloc, it was that a side-table has nowhere to put a new node. Emitting into a caller-lent output arena through `Builder` removes that fault without touching the no-alloc discipline, because the arena is caller-provided on both ends (`arena.rs:35-41`).

The deeper strength is the unification it exposes. `fold_core` (`vehje-codegen/src/lib.rs:86-134`) is already a generic pre-order walk with a per-node `visit`. Grammar (input to IR), expansion (IR to IR), and codegen (IR to bytes) are then one operation, a fold whose sink differs: an arena via `Builder`, or a byte stream via `ByteEmitter`. This is the single-definition collapse the codebase should want. The `FamilyExpand` hook the topic proposes (`202607241330_topic.ir-to-ir-expansion.md:43-48`) completes a now-uniform triple with the existing `FamilyResolve` and `FamilyCheck` (`vehje-typecheck/src/lib.rs:36-47`), each a per-`Raw` extension seam with a Core-only default. That is the correct seam and the correct shape for it.

The effect/binding-time separation is also already correct in principle. `effect.rs:9-13` explicitly retired a lattice that "conflated effect (what world-change) with binding time (when known)" and moved binding time onto its own graded modality. That split is the precondition for everything in part (B) below being expressible.

## Findings on the IR-to-IR architecture (A)

### 1. The fixpoint does not terminate in general, and it fails by exhausting a caller-lent arena

The topic's termination story is "run expansion, if the IR changed run again, until no change" (`topic:33-38`). Const-fold plus branch-drop is strictly size-reducing, so on its own it terminates. Macro expansion is not size-reducing: a macro can expand to a term containing another macro call, and recursive or mutually recursive macros have no fixpoint. This is the classical non-termination of unrestricted partial evaluation and unrestricted macro expansion (Jones, Gomard, Sestoft, *Partial Evaluation and Automatic Program Generation*, 1993, on offline termination and binding-time-analysis-driven safety). The old `Rewrite` could never diverge because every step decreased the index; the emit path removes that guardrail. Worse, in a no-alloc world divergence does not hang cleanly, it exhausts the output arena and surfaces as `Builder` returning `Maybe::Isnt` (`arena.rs:45-54`), which without care reads as a truncated program rather than an error.

Why it matters: this is the exact corner where staging systems have historically been unsound or non-terminating, and it is the first thing to pin as a test.

Direction: give the fixpoint driver an explicit fuel bound (a `Cap`), and separate three outcomes that are currently one: fixpoint reached (root unchanged across a round), budget exhausted (arena full), and fuel exhausted (possible divergence). The last two are diagnostics that name the family whose expansion did not converge, keyed on the span, in the shape of `CheckError` (`vehje-typecheck/src/lib.rs:61-72`). Use the existing `hash_of` over the root (`vehje-lower/src/lib.rs:189`) as the cheap "did it change" signal across rounds, but do not trust hash-equality as the termination oracle: a macro that oscillates A to B to A reports "changed" forever, so the fuel bound is the real backstop, not the hash. The lesson is already in this repo: `DEEPDIVE_LOWERING.md.tmpl:122-149` records that the deferred e-graph's extractor hung on cycles and had to become an iterative work-stack with a bound. The fixpoint expansion driver inherits that lesson verbatim.

### 2. The fixpoint and the deferred equality-saturation stratum are the same operation at two precisions

Expansion enables folding (`if true` appears only after a macro expands to it), and folding enables expansion (a folded branch exposes a macro call in the surviving arm). The passes are mutually enabling, which is precisely the condition that motivates equality saturation (Tate, Stepp, Tatlock, Lerner, *Equality Saturation*, POPL 2009; egg, Willsey et al., 2021). So the cheap fixpoint over {expand, fold} and the deferred `LowerStrategy::Saturate` (`vehje-lower/src/lib.rs:82-86`, `DEEPDIVE_LOWERING.md.tmpl:100-138`) are one mechanism at two costs: the cheap one applies a fixed rule set to a fixpoint by re-emission; the e-graph applies an open rule set to a fixpoint by congruence closure. Naming them as one thing keeps the termination discipline shared. It also settles a design fork by bench rather than intuition (per the workspace's bench-decided-fork rule): outer whole-program re-emission to fixpoint, versus expand-recursively-inside-the-emit-visit (normalization-by-evaluation style, each final node touched once) with an outer round only as a safety re-check. The second is usually cheaper because it avoids re-walking unchanged subtrees, but the fold-enables-expansion coupling means the outer round can still find work, so measure iteration count and arena peak on a macro-heavy corpus before locking.

### 3. Two live arenas, and a caller-declared expansion budget is now part of the contract

Each round emits a whole new arena, so two arenas are live at once (input and output) and the caller must double-buffer and swap roles per round. The caller must size each buffer to the fully-expanded worst case, which is undecidable in general for the same reason termination is. This is the true no-alloc cost of the reframing: the arena-sizing burden becomes a first-class part of the contract, not an afterthought, and the fuel bound from finding 1 is what converts an undecidable size into a declared one (bounded rounds times bounded per-round growth is a bounded total). State the budget in the API: the fixpoint driver takes two arenas plus a fuel `Cap`, and returns which buffer holds the stable arena and the stable root, because resolve and check then run on that buffer (`vehje-typecheck/src/lib.rs:190-197` consumes the arena it is handed, so the buffer identity must be explicit).

### 4. Macro hygiene is the largest unaddressed soundness hole

The topic's `FamilyExpand` returns an expansion root and says nothing about capture. The team clearly knows capture exists: CSE refuses to share any subtree containing a `Var` precisely because two syntactic `Var("x")` can resolve to different binders (`vehje-lower/src/lib.rs:227-238`, `280-282`; `DEEPDIVE_LOWERING.md.tmpl:50-79`). Macro expansion needs the stronger property. When a macro body binds `x` and the call site also has `x` in scope, naive splicing captures, and when the macro body references a free `x` it must see the definition-site `x`, not the use-site one. This is the hygiene problem (Kohlbecker, Friedman, Felleisen, Duba, *Hygienic Macro Expansion*, 1986; Clinger and Rees; and the modern set-of-scopes model, Flatt, *Binding as Sets of Scopes*, POPL 2016).

Why it matters: a macro system without hygiene is unsound, silently, and the failure is a captured variable that typechecks.

Direction: make hygiene an explicit obligation on `FamilyExpand`. The cheapest sound realization in this arena is fresh-name generation: expansion emits binders with freshly-interned `Str` names from a gensym counter threaded through the `Builder`, and because resolve runs *after* expansion (`topic:22`), it re-binds by the fresh names, so capture is structurally impossible. The interner already exists (`vehje-ir/src/intern.rs`), and a monotonic fresh-slot counter is no-alloc. Write the capturing-macro test now, red until hygiene lands, per the catalogue-edge-cases-as-tests discipline; it is the theorem-worth-of-debugging that this corner otherwise costs later.

### 5. Once emit exists, a hash-consing Builder subsumes CSE, and keeping both is drift

The topic keeps the redirect-table CSE for "annotate-in-place" sharing (`topic:49-52`). But once every productive pass emits into a `Builder`, CSE is a property of the Builder, not a separate pass: a hash-consing constructor (Filliatre and Conchon, *Type-Safe Modular Hash-Consing*, 2006) returns an existing node when the structural hash matches on emit, so `const-fold`, `macro-expand`, `anf`, and sharing are all "emit into a hash-consing arena," and sharing falls out for free. The same capture restriction transfers unchanged (intern only variable-free, or work post-resolution with fresh names), so soundness is not lost. Maintaining a redirect table and an emit path as two sharing mechanisms is the duplicated-meaning smell: a fix to one drifts from the other. Recommend folding CSE into the emitting Builder for the productive-pass world and keeping the redirect table only if a bench shows a real win for CSE-without-re-emit on an arena you do not otherwise need to copy. This is a single-definition collapse, and it is measurable.

### 6. `FamilyExpand`'s return type conflates "passed through" with "arena full"

`expand_raw(...) -> Maybe<NodeRef>` (`topic:43-46`) cannot distinguish "this Raw is not a macro, emit it unchanged" from "I tried to expand and ran out of output arena." Those are different control-flow outcomes and the fixpoint driver must react differently (the first is normal, the second is finding 1's budget diagnostic). Use a three-way result (`Expanded(NodeRef)` / `Passthrough` / `Full`), not `Maybe`. Also add a no-progress guard: if expanding family F on a structurally-identical payload yields a structurally-identical Raw (checkable with the existing `hash_of`), refuse it as "macro F did not reduce its input," which catches a common family-author bug at the seam rather than as a hang.

## The compile/runtime line (B): the terms pinned for this framework

The question "where does the compile/runtime line fall" is malformed as a line. The IR already carries the correct structure: `BindingTime` is a four-point lattice, `{LanguageAuthor, Bundler, HostLoader, Runtime}` (`vehje-ir/src/grade.rs:105-115`), and `Knowledge` is a join over it (`grade.rs:119-148`). There are three cut-points, not one, and each cut is a specialization opportunity, a Futamura projection made concrete (Futamura, *Partial Evaluation of Computation Process*, 1971). Here is where each classical term lands.

**Ahead-of-time compilation lives at two binding times.**

- At `LanguageAuthor` time: `vehje-runtime-gen` produces the per-language validated-data package, and the Zig `build.zig` comptime step specializes one hand-authored engine against it (`DEEPDIVE_PACKAGE_MANIFEST.md.tmpl:7-9`, `22-27`, `92-102`). This is the first Futamura projection as a build step: specialize the generic interpreter (the shared Zig engine) with respect to the language definition (the package), yielding a language-specific runtime. It is AOT, performed once per language, by the Zig compiler's comptime evaluator. That the package crosses as data rather than generated source (`MANIFEST:17-27`) is the LMS thesis in a different alphabet: one engine to audit, specialized by staging, not one hand-written compiler per language.

- At `Bundler` time: the Rust compile side lowers, expands, folds, discharges the graded proof, and emits the tier-tagged `Checked` residual (`vehje-codegen/src/lib.rs:34-72`, `vehje/.claude/CLAUDE.md` tier statement). For a statically-known program, everything up to and including the residual is AOT. Literal const-inlining here is specialization of `P(c)` to `P_c`: the residual is no longer parameterized over `c`.

**Load-time compilation (not profile-guided JIT) lives at `HostLoader` time.** A script that arrives after the Bundler build was not known at Bundler time, so the same lowering (the shared `RuleTable` const-fold and CSE, `vehje-lower/src/lib.rs:88-113`, `DEEPDIVE_LOWERING.md.tmpl:177-186`) must run inside the Zig runtime at load. This is exactly the debt `vehje-runtime-abi/DESIGN.md.tmpl:19-22` names: "the residual is a control-flow-graph-of-blocks program... the debt that the runtime contains a compile stage lands." The precise term is load-time compilation (the R2R / install-time-compile family), not tracing/profiling JIT: the current design lowers once at load and then executes, it does not recompile hot regions guided by runtime profile. `RuleTable` being the same data on both sides (`DEEPDIVE_LOWERING.md.tmpl:177-186`) is the mechanism that keeps the Bundler-time specializer and the HostLoader-time specializer from drifting: it is one specializer replayed at two binding times.

**Interpretation lives at `Runtime` time and is one end of the output spectrum, not a compile decision.** `Tier::Arena` walks the serialized residual directly (`DEEPDIVE_DISPATCH_AND_DECODE.md.tmpl:19-29`). It is a way of executing the already-specialized residual, orthogonal to when the specialization happened.

### Does literal const-inlining mandate a compile step?

Yes, but not a *native* one, and this is the crux. Const-inlining is specialization: it produces a residual `P_c` distinct from the general `P`. It does not mandate native codegen; the residual `P_c` is still an IR arena and can be interpreted (`Tier::Arena`). So a *fully interpreted execution model is coherent after const-inlining*: you interpret the specialized residual instead of the general program. What const-inlining mandates is that the specialization *stage exist at the binding time the constant becomes known*. If `c` is known at Bundler time, the stage is AOT in Rust. If `c` only becomes known when a script arrives, the stage must run at load time in Zig. Therefore:

You can be fully interpreted at the `Runtime` binding time, but you cannot be fully interpreted across the whole pipeline. Const-inlining and macro expansion are a compile stage that must exist at the binding time the inputs become known, and for runtime-arriving scripts that binding time is inside the runtime. The interpreter always interprets a compiled (specialized) residual; there is no configuration with no compile stage at all once you commit to literal inlining and expansion. The "line" is the binding-time lattice, and the compile stage is replicated at each stage that is not the last. The two-artifact split follows because one artifact (Zig) straddles two binding times (`HostLoader` compile and `Runtime` execute), so it must contain a compiler. The artifact boundary (two) is coarser than the binding-time boundary (four); that coarseness *is* the "runtime contains a compile stage" debt, and it is principled, not incidental.

### The axis the framework already has but does not yet use

`BindingTime` is declared and inert. The check pass sets `binding: Knowledge::empty(), assurance: Assurance::Unclaimed` unconditionally (`vehje-typecheck/src/lib.rs:305-313`), and const-fold folds only *syntactic* literals (`Node::Lit(Literal::Bool(_))` at `vehje-lower/src/lib.rs:155`). That is constant folding, a peephole, not partial evaluation. A staged const-fold folds a node when its inputs' `Knowledge` grade includes the current stage's binding time, whether or not they are syntactic literals: a config value known at `Bundler` time is inline-able even though it is not written as a literal. This is precisely the LMS `Rep[T]` versus `T` distinction (Rompf and Odersky, *Lightweight Modular Staging*, GPCE 2010), rendered as a graded modality: a value graded `{LanguageAuthor, Bundler}` is a present-now `T`, inline it; a value graded `{Runtime}` is a `Rep[T]`, emit code for it. The framework has independently reinvented a four-level version of the two-level binding-time discipline (Nielson and Nielson; MetaML/MetaOCaml, Taha and Sheard, *MetaML and multi-stage programming*, 2000). Wiring the discharge is what turns the declared axis into a working specializer, and it is the load-bearing next step for part (B) to be real rather than latent.

### Macro expansion has a binding-time constraint that const-fold does not

This is the finding that binds (A) and (B). Const-fold rules are *data* (`RuleTable`), so they port to Zig and can run at `HostLoader` time. A `FamilyExpand` hook is *Rust code*, and Rust never runs in the runtime (`vehje/.claude/CLAUDE.md`). So a runtime-arriving script cannot expand a family macro at load time unless the expansion is also reified into the package as data. Two coherent resolutions: (a) family macros are pinned to binding time no later than `Bundler`, so a script arriving at `Runtime` may not carry an un-discharged family macro; or (b) `FamilyExpand` is expressible as a shipped rewrite-rule table, not arbitrary Rust, so the Zig load-time stage can replay it. Resolution (a) is almost certainly right, and the design already implies it: `MacroExpand` is "a compile-stage handler dispatch" whose effect "is discharged here, so the residual carries no unexpanded macro" (`vehje-lower/src/lib.rs:395-408`), and the twelfth Core form unifies "the effect set, the host-call boundary, and macro expansion into one handler discipline" (`vehje-ir/src/node.rs:131-143`). A macro is an algebraic-effect operation at build-time binding, discharged by expansion, exactly as a `Handle` clause discharges an effect (`vehje-typecheck/src/lib.rs:296-303`, whose FIXME to subtract handled operations from the residual effect is the same subtraction). State it as a binding-time constraint: a family macro operation carries binding time no later than `Bundler`, and the effect proof discharged in Rust before lowering is what guarantees no unexpanded macro crosses the ABI. That is the soundness statement, and it is the same guarantee the effect axis already provides for build-environment effects. `Interp` (`vehje-ir/src/node.rs:122-124`, "a distinct form because the staging rule attaches to it") is the natural first site to demonstrate binding-time-directed emission: a known-now interpolated value inlines into the generated text, an unknown-now one stays a runtime interpolation, which is the splice/escape of a two-level quasiquote.

## Verdict on the tier model and the output spectrum

Keep the tier narrowing; it is correct and bench-supported. `Tier` names only the internal execution form, `{Arena, Bytecode}`, and native is pushed off the tier axis onto the output spectrum, chosen per region by grade (`vehje-runtime-abi/DESIGN.md.tmpl:37-40`, `DEEPDIVE_DISPATCH_AND_DECODE.md.tmpl:19-29`). The bench evidence forces this: native buys 1.0x to 1.5x over a good interpreter on scalar code (`DEEPDIVE_OUTPUT_SPECTRUM.md.tmpl:81-92`), so committing a whole program to a native tier tag would price in a whole-program native-versus-managed decision the evidence says does not pay. Native as a per-region point on the spectrum is the honest shape. The plain-switch-beats-threading finding (1.7x, `OUTPUT_SPECTRUM:94-103`) and the tier-dependent branch strategy (`OUTPUT_SPECTRUM:105-115`) are consistent with this: the interpreter floor is close enough to native that the specialization effort should be spent on the binding-time axis (fold more at earlier stages) rather than on a native tier, for the common scalar consumer.

The one sharpening: the codegen deepdive presents "interpretation to transpilation" as one axis (`OUTPUT_SPECTRUM:6-19`), which quietly folds binding time into the spectrum. They are two orthogonal axes plus the tier, three in total: the tier is the execution form (`Arena` vs `Bytecode`); the spectrum is how much you reduce versus re-express at a given stage (interpret-to-value vs transpile-to-source); binding time is *when* the stage runs. A transpilation target is a `Bundler`-time full-residualization that defers all reduction to the target language's own runtime; an interpretation target is a `Runtime`-time full-reduction. `Knowledge` (`grade.rs:119`) is the third axis and it is already in the IR; the codegen contract simply does not reference it yet. Name it as the orthogonal third axis and the model is the right shape.

## Open questions handed forward

1. Fixpoint termination policy: the fuel `Cap` value and shape, oscillation handling, and arena-budget exhaustion as a named diagnostic rather than a silent truncation. Plus the bench-decided fork: outer whole-program re-emission to fixpoint versus expand-recursively-in-the-visit with an outer safety round, measured on a macro-heavy corpus for iteration count and arena peak.

2. Macro hygiene: gensym-through-the-`Builder` versus a set-of-scopes discipline in a no-alloc interned-name arena. This is the biggest open soundness item; nothing in the current design addresses it.

3. Binding-time discharge: wire `Knowledge`/`BindingTime` so const-fold becomes binding-time-directed (partial evaluation), not syntactic-literal folding. Decide where the discharge runs (inside `check`, or a separate binding-time pass before lower) and the join semantics of the four-point lattice for partial bundling.

4. Whether to collapse CSE into a hash-consing `Builder` and retire the redirect table for the productive-pass world (bench the CSE-without-re-emit case before deciding).

5. The `HostLoader` load-time stage: confirm it runs the same expansion-and-fold as `Bundler`, and confirm the binding-time constraint on family macros (no un-discharged family macro crosses the ABI). If any consumer genuinely needs runtime-arriving macros, that forces `FamilyExpand`-as-shipped-data, which is a larger commitment; the effect proof should refuse the case until then.

6. Whether a profiling JIT to the native point on the spectrum is ever worth building given the 1.0x to 1.5x scalar ceiling, or whether the effort belongs entirely on the binding-time axis (fold more, earlier). The evidence currently points at the latter for the common consumer, with vectorising native as a rare compute-bound opt-in.

## Addendum: auditing the intended shape (every FIXME assumed built as the design intends)

Per op's steer, this audits the design's intended concrete shape, with every FIXME imagined implemented as the oracle intends, not the honestly-half-built state. So "not built yet" is not itself a finding. The question is whether the intended shape is correct, and where the intent as documented is inaccurate, self-contradicting, or leaves a mechanism the design already owns unused. Sources: the round-closing consolidated topic (`202607240100`), the bench-remeasure (`202607220300`) and full-arc findings (`202607240015`), and the `synth_*` corpus. Ordered: the one that is a real bug if built to the documented intent first, then the intended-shape design findings.

### 0. Built to the documented intent, the per-slice manifest hash is unsound

This is the single place where implementing the FIXME as written would introduce a bug. `DEEPDIVE_PACKAGE_MANIFEST.md.tmpl:58-59` and the oracle (`202607240100:267-268,288`) both say the manifest content-addresses "over `vehje-ir`'s one structural-hash primitive," per-slice and whole-package. The whole-package hash shipped correctly over the byte-image (`arvo_hash::xxhash3_64`, `vehje-runtime-gen/src/lib.rs:116-124`), silently diverging from the doc. The per-slice half is FIXME'd (`runtime-gen/src/lib.rs:77-81`). An implementer building that FIXME to the documented intent would reach for the structural hash the prose names, and that is unsound: `vehje-ir/src/hash.rs:1-14` states the structural word-fold is interner-local and NOT interner-stable, and that cross-artifact identity is the byte-image hash's job "precisely because the byte-image is interner-independent" (`vehje-fixpoint/src/incr.rs:7,75-79` uses it for the same reason). The manifest exists to prove the Zig runtime specialized from exactly this package, which crosses the interner boundary, so it must use the byte-image hash. The intent as documented names the primitive that would break it. Fix the prose in the deepdive and the oracle to say byte-image (interner-independent) content hash, and reserve "structural hash" for the within-compilation consumer (hash-consing in lower). This is the same category error part B flags between tier and binding time, in the hash layer: a within-stage identity is not a cross-stage identity.

### 1. The binding-time grade should be thermometer-encoded, so illegal binding-time sets are unrepresentable

The intended discharge rule is multi-cogen: "discharge each property at the earliest binding time its inputs are known" (`202607240100:104`, from Glueck-Joergensen, `synth_binding-time:15-30`). That rule is well-defined only when "earliest all inputs available" is a single point. Binding-time availability is monotone: a value known at `LanguageAuthor` is known at every later stage. So the correct grade is a thermometer over the four ordered sources, exactly the shape `EffectMask` already uses ("Thermometer-shaped so the lattice join is a bitwise union," `grade.rs:22-24`). But `Knowledge` is documented only as "a join over the knowledge sources" (`grade.rs:119-121`) with no thermometer discipline, so as an arbitrary subset mask it admits meaningless grades like `{LanguageAuthor, Runtime}` without `{Bundler, HostLoader}`, on which "the earliest binding time" is ill-defined. Intent fix: make `Knowledge` thermometer-encoded like `EffectMask`, so known-early-implies-known-late is structural, illegal binding-time sets are unrepresentable (the cross-cutting synth thread, `synth_00_index:52-54`), and "earliest known" is the lowest set bit, well-defined, with the discharge point the max over a node's inputs. This is the difference between the four stages being a genuine chain (which the staging story needs) and a raw powerset (which the current type admits).

### 2. Binding-time-directed reduction is one mechanism the intended design splits three ways

The design cites Nielson-Nielson, where every form carries a binding-time annotation and "the interpolation form and the branch forms are binding-time boundaries" (`synth_binding-time:32-44`). The intended Core honors this only for `Interp` (marked "a staging rule attaches to it... now a binding-time grade," `node.rs:122-124`, `202607240100:144-146`), while branch folding lives in `vehje-lower`'s const-fold and macro expansion lives in the `Handle`/`FamilyExpand` discipline. These are three faces of one operation: discharge a form when its inputs are known at the current stage. `If` with a static condition folds (part A const-fold); `Interp` with a static value inlines (the staging rule); a macro (a build-stage `Handle` operation) expands. `GradeTable` already carries a per-node binding slot (`grade.rs:191-202`), so the machinery is per-form already. The intended shape should name binding-time-directed reduction as one pass over all forms, of which If-folding, Interp-inlining, and macro-expansion are instances, rather than three separately-sited mechanisms. This is the LMS collapse (one staging discipline, not per-form special cases), and it is the concrete meaning of "the compile/runtime line is the binding-time lattice" from part B: the lattice drives one reducer, not three.

### 3. Macro-expansion-as-Handle-discharge is the IR-to-IR emit path; the intended unification closes the loop

The strongest piece of the intended design is the `Handle` unification: an effect is an operation, a handler services it, a host-call is a runtime-effect the host handles, and macro expansion is a compile-stage-effect the compile stage handles, with "which stage provides the handler the binding-time coordinate" (`node.rs:131-143`, `202607240100:153-156`). Assumed built, this is correct and unusually clean. The intent-level synthesis it implies, and should state: a handler's discharge is binding-time-polymorphic in its output kind. A build-time handler discharges by emitting IR (generative, into a `Builder`), a runtime handler discharges by producing a value (reduction, into a value-arena). So the part-A `FamilyExpand` hook and the build-time `Handle` discharge are the same mechanism, both "fold with a `Builder` sink," and the design carries them as two seams (`FamilyExpand` in lower, `Handle` discharge in check) that are one. Naming them as one build-time-handler-emits-IR mechanism removes an apparent duplication and makes the IR-to-IR path the concrete realization of build-stage effect discharge. The value-arena producer (`DEEPDIVE_VALUE_TRANSPORT`) and the expansion emitter are then the runtime-stage and build-stage instances of one emit discipline, which is why both are child-before-parent arenas.

### 4. The two-stage inclusion proof discharges one obligation two ways; "the same witness" is imprecise across the artifact boundary

Assumed built (the #29 program-derived sets), the intended proof has a compile-time path (a `ContainsAll` type bound minting `Checked<T>` for static programs, `codegen/src/lib.rs:61-72`) and a runtime path (a bitmask subset test in the Zig runtime for arriving scripts, the certifying-compilation shape from `synth_binding-time:78-88`). `codegen:58-61` says the runtime half "produces the same `Checked` witness through a checked path." But `Checked<'a, T>` is a Rust typestate (`typecheck/src/lib.rs:80-107`); it cannot exist in the Zig runtime, which has no Rust types. The two paths discharge the same obligation (the program's families and effects are included in the target's declared sets) in two forms: a compile-time type bound versus a runtime pass/fail bool. They are not the same witness. The intended framing should say one obligation, two realizations (a typestate at `Bundler`, a re-derived-and-re-checked bitmask at `HostLoader`), which is exactly the certifying-versus-certified distinction the synth banked, rather than a single witness crossing the artifact boundary.

### 5. Lift proof-directed strategy selection from codegen to the relational engine

The output side already selects strategy per region by evidence (interpret / bytecode / native, "the strategy is chosen by evidence about the region," `DEEPDIVE_OUTPUT_SPECTRUM:105-115`). The intended relational engine instead fixes whole-column as the base with semi-naive deferred (`202607240100:280-289`, framework-completion "the measured winner"). The remeasure shows that choice is shape-dependent: semi-naive wins 2x to 5x on deep-narrow graphs, whole-column ~1.2x on wide-shallow-many-target ones, the margin growing with scale (`202607220300` C5). So the same discipline the design already owns on the output side applies to the engine: select whole-column versus semi-naive by the graph's depth and fan-out, measured, rather than fixing one. The intended shape leaves this lever unused; it is the identical "select by evidence about the region" pattern, applied to the fixpoint rather than the emit.

### 6. The native-per-region-point decision rests on a self-contradicting number in the round's own locked evidence

Assumed built (the copy-and-patch stencil trusted base), whether it earns its place depends on the native margin, and the round's evidence contradicts itself. The remeasure retracts the old ceiling ("The 1.2x headline is retracted," `202607220300:19-27`) to ~2.0x/2.2x, controlling the interpreter-partial-eval artifact by crossing FFI-opaque; the later full-arc findings re-state 1.0-1.2x (`202607240015:68-78`), controlling the native-auto-vectorisation artifact. Opposite artifacts, opposite-direction corrections, no reconciliation, and the oracle propagated the 1.0-1.5x side (`202607240100:419`, `DEEPDIVE_OUTPUT_SPECTRUM:81-92`). The intended positioning (native a per-region point, the stencil a marginal opt-in, not a keystone) survives either number qualitatively, since even 2.2x is not the 10x that would make native the driver, so the tier narrowing in my part-B verdict holds. But the specific margin that decides "whether native beats the cheap-lowered interpreter enough to justify the stencil trusted base" (`202607240100:426`, an explicitly open item) is unsettled, and the remeasure also reattributes the switch-beats-threading result to the Zig 0.16 calling convention rather than to vehje's op vocabulary as `OUTPUT_SPECTRUM:94-103` claims. Direction: one bench controlling both artifacts at once, before the stencil's justification is treated as settled.

### Net

The intended shape is correct and unusually coherent; the `Handle` unification (finding 3), the graded proof spine, the inclusion-not-coverage contract, and the binding-time lattice as the compile/runtime structure are the right calls. The genuine intent-level issues are: the documented manifest hash would be unsound if built as written (finding 0, the one real bug); `Knowledge` should be thermometer-encoded so illegal binding-time sets are unrepresentable (finding 1); binding-time-directed reduction is one mechanism the design sites in three places and should unify (findings 2 and 3), which is also the concrete content of "the compile/runtime line is the binding-time lattice"; the two-stage inclusion proof is two discharges of one obligation, not one witness (finding 4); proof-directed selection should extend to the engine (finding 5); and the native margin the stencil rests on is self-contradicting in the locked evidence (finding 6). One note on classification, not on the intended shape itself: the `MacroExpand`, Core const-fold, and `Anf` FIXMEs were specified by the `202607241330` topic (emit into a fresh arena), so the earlier deferral audit's "open-design, shape unspecified" label (`202607241300`, Class 3) is superseded; their intended shape is the part-A emit path, and it is buildable now, not open.
