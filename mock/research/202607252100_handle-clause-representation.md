# The Handle clause representation, and how an operation is performed

**Date:** 2026-07-25
**Status:** research. A design analysis for the round that lands `Handle`'s clause representation; not a locked round.
**Scope:** questions 1 to 5 as posed: the clause record, the perform form, separability of the non-resuming discharge, the loop forms, and the soundness obligations under no-alloc.
**Source:** `mock/research/canon/the-soul-of-vehje-positive-catalogue.md` (frontier B and C), `mock/research/202607251530_language-completeness-census.md`, the shipped Core (`vehje-ir/src/node.rs`), the shipped tree-walk (`mock/runtime-zig/src/runtime.zig`), the wire (`vehje-runtime-abi/src/wire/serialize.rs`), and the four passes.

**Verdict:** the Core is missing the introduction form of its own effect algebra, so `Handle` gets a named clause record in a fourth caller-lent region carrying an operation `Sym`, a resumption binder, an operand arity, and a body; a thirteenth form `Perform { op, args }` introduces the operation; the operation identity is a generatively-fresh `Sym` resolved lexically, which makes the non-resuming discharge a statically-bound unwind that costs nothing on any path a program does not take.

The shape of the finding is worth stating before the detail. `Handle` today is an eliminator with no matching introduction: nothing in the twelve forms can build a term that performs an operation, so the eliminator has nothing to eliminate. That is not a census gap of the kind the negative catalogue rules out (`the-inverse-of-vehje-negative-catalogue.md:30`, "twelve-forms-complete-by-algebra"). It is an incompleteness in the algebra itself. In Plotkin and Pretnar's presentation the free model of a signature is generated by the operation symbols and the handler is the unique homomorphism out of it; shipping the homomorphism without the generators is half an algebra. So the argument for a new form here is the same kind of argument that admitted `Handle`, and it passes the same bar.

The second framing point is the one that decides most of the operational questions. Every construct the arriving consumer needs (`return`, `break`, `break v`, `continue`, `?`) is a *non-resuming* discharge whose handler is *lexically* determined at the point the front end emits it. Lexical plus non-resuming is the fragment where an effect handler is not a control-flow mystery at all: it is a labelled exit, the same thing WebAssembly's `br` to an enclosing labelled block is, and it can be compiled to an unwind with a statically known target. The whole design below is an attempt to take exactly that fragment, price it honestly, and reserve the slots the hard fragment will need without paying for them now.

## 1. The clause representation

A clause carries an operation identity, a resumption binder, an operand arity, a body, and a span. The design record already names three of those (`docs/DESIGN-DEEP-DIVES.md:456-462`: "an operation id, a handler-body handle, and a resumption binder rather than a bare child ref"); the arity and the span are additions this analysis argues for below.

**Where it lives: a fourth caller-lent region, not a `Node` variant and not the child pool.** The IR is three regions today (`vehje-ir/src/arena.rs:39-41`: nodes, spans, pool). A clause record region is a fourth of exactly the same shape, and `Handle { body, clauses: ClauseList }` carries a `{ start, len }` into it the way `NodeList` carries one into the pool (`node.rs:38-41`).

The alternatives and what they cost. Encoding clauses in the existing child pool means the pool holds two kinds of thing, since the pool element type is `NodeRef` (`arena.rs:31`) and an operation identity is a `Sym`; every scheme that fits a `Sym` into that pool is a pun. The nearest pun is the one the value arena just adopted for record fields, alternating a key node with a value node (the state file's "a record names its fields through its children"), which here would alternate a `Var(op)` node with the clause body. That works, costs nothing on the wire, and puts a `Var` (a reference) in a declaration position, which is the class of thing that later reads as a bug. The other alternative is a thirteenth `Node::Clause` variant that never evaluates. It gains a span and a structural hash for free (`vehje-ir/src/hash.rs`), and it costs the property that every term-walking pass currently relies on: `resolve`, `check`, `anf`, and `encode` all iterate `arena.list(clauses)` treating each element as a term (`vehje-resolve/src/lib.rs:191-193`, `vehje-typecheck/src/lib.rs:301-303`, `vehje-lower/src/anf.rs:139-141`, `vehje-runtime-abi/src/encode.rs:206-210`), and a non-term in the term arena means each of them acquires a skip rule. One region holding two kinds of thing is the failure the separate region avoids.

The cost of the fourth region is real and should be named: the host sizes and lends one more buffer, and every constructor and pass signature that threads the arena threads it too. That is the fourth instance of a cost already paid three times, and it is the cost PE3's mandated mitigations exist for (size-refusing constructors, `must_use` on the exhaustion result).

**The fields, and why each is authored rather than derived.**

| Field | Type | Why it cannot be derived |
|---|---|---|
| `op` | `Sym` | The operation's identity. Not recoverable from the body. |
| `resume` | `Sym` | The resumption binder. Its presence is the reservation the multi-shot case needs; see section 3. |
| `arity` | `Uint<8, Hot>` | Operand count. Counting `Lambda` nesting in the body is not equivalent, since a body may return a lambda. |
| `body` | `NodeRef` | The handler term, a `Lambda` chain over the operands. |
| `span` | `Span` | The span table is parallel to the node arena (`arena.rs:39-41`), so a clause outside that arena has no span unless it carries one, and a clause is exactly where several of section 5's diagnostics land. |

Operands curry through the body's `Lambda` chain rather than getting binder slots in the clause record. That reuses machinery that already works end to end: `resolve` pushes the frame (`vehje-resolve/src/lib.rs:142-145`), the binder rule already drops the parameter's reach slot (`vehje-typecheck/src/lib.rs:262-267`), ANF already normalises a `Lambda` body as its own evaluation context (`anf.rs:127-130`), and the runtime already binds one parameter per argument (`runtime.zig:306-314`). The resumption does *not* curry, because it is not an ordinary value and the check wants a syntactic grip on it (section 3).

**What is deliberately not a field.** The node comment says a clause carries "an operation, a handler body with access to the resumption, and the discharge" (`node.rs:134-135`). The discharge is not authorable data. Under frontier B the discharge is which stage provides the handler, and that is the binding-time coordinate (`vehje-ir/src/grade.rs:106-115`), currently hard-coded in the check (`vehje-typecheck/src/lib.rs:311-315`). Authoring it on the clause would author a derived quantity. The correction to the comment: two things are authored, and the third is the grade the spine already owns.

**On the wire.** A clause is four words in its own section, after the string blob: `op`, `resume`, `arity`, `body`. `Sym` already serialises as its 32 bits including the 3-bit domain tag (`serialize.rs:228-232`), so a minted operation identity round-trips unchanged. The node record is untouched: `Handle` keeps `body` in payload word 1 and the clause list start and length in words 2 and 3, inside the seven-word record (`serialize.rs:48-51`). The encoder gains one method beside `pool` (`encode.rs:104-105`), and the header gains a clause count word, which is a `VERSION` bump (`serialize.rs:44`) that costs nothing while no consumer holds an image.

## 2. How an operation is performed

**The conclusion: a distinct form, `Perform { op: Sym, args: NodeList }`.** Wire tag 12, four words used of seven.

The three candidates, priced.

*Through `Raw`, with handler lookup preceding the host call.* `Raw` carries a `FamilyId` and a payload (`node.rs:131`), and has no slot for an operation identity, so the identity would ride inside the payload as a literal, which is the bare-value-hiding-a-role failure the workspace rules name. Worse, it conflates two axes. A family is a vocabulary and many family operations are pure: arithmetic lowers to `Raw` (`202607251149_the-first-party-language-shape.md:33`) and must not acquire an effect, because an effect on `1 + 2` is a grade the handler discipline then has to subtract everywhere, which is precisely the effect-system tax the design cannot afford. Rejected on that ground, not on ergonomics.

*As application of a bound operation value.* The `Handle` binds each operation name as a value, and `perform op(x)` is `Apply(Var(op), [x])`. This is the capability spelling, and it has the best pedigree of the three (Brachthäuser, Schuster and Ostermann, "Effects as capabilities", OOPSLA 2020; the second-class restriction in Effekt is exactly what keeps it sound). It adds no Core form at all, and the resolution falls out of the binder machinery unchanged. Its costs are operational and they land in the wrong place. The runtime would evaluate the callee and then switch on the value kind at every `Apply` (`runtime.zig:309-312` currently refuses anything but a closure), which taxes the hot path to serve the cold one. The CFG producer would have to treat every `Apply` as a potential terminator rather than cutting a block at a syntactically visible form. The check's effect inference, which is a syntactic bottom-up fold today (`vehje-typecheck/src/lib.rs:220-321`), would depend on the resolution side-table, coupling two passes that are currently independent. And a capability that is a value can escape its handler's dynamic extent, which is only sound if the lease axis enforces non-escape, and `UnplaceableLease` is declared and never constructed anywhere in the workspace (`vehje-typecheck/src/lib.rs:69` is its only occurrence).

*A distinct form.* One more variant, one more wire tag, one more arm in each of the four passes and the interpreter. In exchange the runtime distinguishes a call from an exit before evaluating anything, the CFG producer cuts blocks syntactically, the effect fold stays syntactic, and the operation is not a value so it cannot escape by construction.

Note what this does *not* forbid. The capability spelling is a perfectly good surface and lowering convention: a front end may write `Apply(Var(op), args)` and the lowering may rewrite it to `Perform` once resolution has named the clause. That is prove-then-erase applied to a spelling, and it costs nothing at runtime because the capability is erased before emission. The Core needs the form; the surface does not have to show it.

**The operation identity is a `Sym` from a fresh generative domain, not a small dense id.** The reason is `break 'outer`. If an operation is drawn from a fixed vocabulary, two loops in the same function share the `break` operation and the inner handler catches the outer's exit. If the front end mints a fresh operation per handler site, labelled break and labelled continue fall out with no additional mechanism, and an unlabelled `break` binds to the innermost enclosing handler because that is the first frame whose clause list matches. The machinery exists: `Generator<D>` mints fresh `Sym`s (`hilavitkutin-sym/src/generator.rs:57`), `SymKind` is a 3-bit tag with `0b010` through `0b111` free (`hilavitkutin-sym/src/kind.rs:4-11`), and the disjointness of a minted domain from source names is structural and already tested (`vehje-ir/src/binder.rs:14-20`, `binder.rs:58-65`). An `OpDomain` is one constant. This is the generative-prompt mechanism of Dybvig, Peyton Jones and Sabry (JFP 2007) reduced to an integer.

**Resolution is lexical, and that is where the cost goes to zero.** `resolve` gains a handler scope alongside the binder scope: a `Handle` pushes its clause operations, a `Perform` resolves its operation against that chain, and the resolution side-table records the perform's node index against the `Handle`'s `NodeRef` (`vehje-resolve/src/lib.rs:207-230`, which maps node index to binder `NodeRef` and needs no change to do it). The clause within that handler is then selected by comparing the operation word, which the runtime must do anyway to pick an arm. Because the handler is statically bound, no evidence vector is threaded through calls. Koka's evidence-passing translation (Xie and Leijen, ICFP 2020 and 2021) exists to make a *dynamically* scoped handler search into a known jump; the lexical fragment does not need to buy that, and the tail-resumptive optimisation those papers land on is the same observation from the other end. Zhang and Myers ("Abstraction-safe effect handlers via tunneling", POPL 2019) is the statement of why static binding is also the *safe* choice: it is what stops a caller's operation being caught by a callee's handler.

**What this costs a program that never uses it.** One `Node` variant, one wire tag, one interpreter switch arm never taken. No allocation, no per-call overhead, no per-frame state. Handlers exist only where the surface writes one of the five constructs, so a program with no early exit contains no `Handle` and no `Perform` and pays nothing.

**What one early exit costs, in the tree-walk.** The perform evaluates its operands left to right and returns an unwind carrier (one word of operation identity plus the existing `Value` union) up the Zig call stack. Each enclosing `Handle` compares the carrier's operation against its clause list, which is `n` integer compares for `n` clauses, typically one. Nothing is copied: a string `Value` is a slice into the residual blob or the session scratch and both outlive the run (`runtime.zig:86-89`), a compound is an index into the lent value arena (`runtime.zig:91-93`), and the environment arena is bump-allocated and never popped (`runtime.zig:13-17`, `152-157`), so every binding a captured value names is still live after the unwind. The discipline that looked like a cost is what makes the unwind free. The handled result then applies the clause body to the operands through the existing machinery, pushing `arity` bindings. Total: no allocation, and integer compares bounded by handler nesting depth, on a path the program only takes when it exits early.

## 3. Whether the non-resuming case separates from the resuming one

It separates, and it should be separated, and the separation costs exactly one reserved field.

**Why it separates.** The non-resuming discharge is the unwinding fragment, and unwinding needs no continuation object of any kind. It needs three things, all of which already hold: evaluation is strict and left to right, so everything the abandoned frames were going to do has either happened or is being abandoned deliberately; nothing runs on the way out, because there is no `finally` clause in the design yet; and no memory is reclaimed on the way out, because the environment and value arenas are bump regions that are never popped. The resuming discharge needs a representation of the rest of the computation, and that representation is an open bench-decided fork that op ordered run now (canon frontier C, `the-soul-of-vehje-positive-catalogue.md:25`: segmented capture-and-reinstate over a host-lent budget against compile-time CPS or defunctionalisation of the handled body). Coupling them means the arriving consumer's `return` and `break` wait on a bench about continuation capture, which is the wrong dependency direction: the cheap fragment blocked behind the expensive one.

**What the representation must commit to now so it does not foreclose the hard case.** One thing: the resumption binder slot on the clause record. If clauses ship as `{ op, arity, body }` and gain `resume` later, every clause record widens, the wire section width changes, and every image built before the change is invalid. One word now is free; one word later is a format break. The binder is left unbound at runtime and no program may reference it until CR1 lands.

**What the representation must *not* commit to.** How a resumption is represented. Because `resume` is a `Sym` naming a binder, its runtime value is whatever the settled representation says: under compile-time CPS or defunctionalisation it is an ordinary closure, which the runtime already has (`runtime.zig:73-75`, `94`); under segmented capture-and-reinstate it is a runtime-internal handle, which gets the treatment `closure` already has, namely a value that cannot cross the ABI (`runtime.zig:79-81`, refused at `runtime.zig:425`). Neither branch of the fork is foreclosed and neither is presumed. Likewise the budget: SK2 demonstrated the fit check as a comptime bound over the lent budget (`mock/research/sketches/202607210430_sk2_bounded-multishot-handle/findings.md`), which is a property of the generated runtime, not per-clause data, so it does not belong on the record either.

**What separation costs.** One field reserved and left unbound. One named refusal in the check for a clause whose body references its resumption, with a catalogued red test asserting the intended behaviour that goes green when CR1 lands. One later addition to the unwind carrier when a resuming discharge needs to return control inward rather than outward. Nothing is rebuilt.

**What doing them together would cost.** The entire non-local control flow surface, which is the widest remaining language gap after the compound escape, waits on a bench about continuation capture whose result changes nothing about the clause record. That is building the cheap thing twice by not building it once.

**The one foreclosure worth naming.** If a later round adds a `finally` or a resource-release clause, the unwind must run code at each frame it passes. A tree-walk that propagates a carrier through the return path can do that; an implementation that jumps directly to the handler cannot. The implementation must therefore be a propagated carrier, not a jump, and that is not merely a preference: it is a property that must be preserved from the first landing.

## 4. The loop forms

No new Core form is needed for `loop`, `while`, `break`, `break v`, or `continue`. The census is right about that (`202607251530_language-completeness-census.md:59-61`). What it does not say is that the encoding is correct denotationally and unusable operationally today, for two reasons that are quantifiable.

**The encodings.**

| Surface | Encoding | Handler placement |
|---|---|---|
| `return e` | `Perform(ret, [e])` | around the enclosing `Lambda` body |
| `break` / `break v` | `Perform(brk_L, [unit])` / `Perform(brk_L, [v])` | around the whole loop, one fresh operation per loop |
| `continue` | `Perform(cont_L, [])` | around one iteration's body, clause body is the loop call |
| `loop { B }` | `let rec go = \_ -> B; go () in go ()` | none of its own |
| `while c { B }` | `loop { if c then B else Perform(brk_L, []) }` | as `loop` |
| `?` | `Perform(thr, [e])` on the error branch | around the enclosing `Lambda` body |

Two of these are worth spelling out. `continue` needs a handler nested *inside* the `break` handler and outside the iteration body, and its clause body is the recursive call, so it is also non-resuming: it discharges by starting the next iteration rather than by returning. That nesting is what makes the requirement in section 2 on exact operation identity load-bearing rather than decorative: a `break` performed inside the iteration body must pass through the `continue` handler untouched, which happens only if the frame's match on the operation word is exact. `?` is doubly blocked, on this mechanism and on the pattern round (task #43), because its natural lowering matches on the outcome value; a family predicate plus `If` reaches it earlier at the cost of a lowering that has to be revisited.

**The first operational obstacle: no tail-call elimination.** The interpreter evaluates a closure body by native Zig recursion (`runtime.zig:313`), and that call is not in tail position within the argument loop, so a self-recursive loop function grows the native stack per iteration. Converting `eval` to a trampoline (a body in tail position becomes a `continue` of an outer loop with the node index and environment updated) is the fix, and it is Steele's argument in "Lambda: the ultimate GOTO" applied to a tree-walk.

**The second, which binds first: the environment arena never pops.** `Env.push` bumps and there is no pop (`runtime.zig:152-157`), by design, so that a captured environment stays readable for the whole run (`runtime.zig:13-17`). The lent region is 1024 slots (`runtime.zig:437`). A loop body with `k` bindings burns at least `k + 1` slots per iteration, so a `while` terminates with `EnvFull` after roughly `1024 / (k + 1)` iterations. For a three-binding body that is about 256 iterations. This is the number that matters, and the good news inside it is that the failure is a named refusal rather than a crash (`runtime.zig:101`).

Reclaiming an iteration's bindings requires proving nothing captured them, which is the reachability the check already computes (`vehje-ir/src/grade.rs:63-99`). That is the same derivation the state file already flags as worth a sketch for the reuse verdict: reach answers "what does this node reach", and both reclamation and reuse want its inverse. One analysis, two consumers.

**One consequence for the CFG producer.** Encoding a loop as `let rec` plus `Apply` hands the block model a `Call` terminator and a function boundary where it wants a back-edge with block arguments (`202607251003_cfg-of-blocks-lowering-design.md:45`). The producer therefore owes a self-tail-call to back-edge rule. That is standard, but it is owed, and it is the reason a dedicated `Loop` form is not obviously wrong; the reason to still refuse one is that the rule is needed anyway for every other self-tail-recursive function, so a `Loop` form would buy a special case for something the general rule already covers.

## 5. Soundness obligations

**Every performed operation resolves to a lexically enclosing clause, or to an operation the target permits.** Otherwise it is a compile error naming the operation, not a runtime refusal. This is what lets the runtime treat "the unwind reached the root unhandled" as an internal-invariant violation rather than a program outcome. `ForbiddenEffect` is declared in the check and never constructed (`vehje-typecheck/src/lib.rs:66` is its only occurrence in that crate); this obligation is what makes it real.

**An operation must not escape its handler's dynamic extent, and this is an axis interaction, not a scoping rule.** `Perform` is not a value, so a *direct* escape is impossible by construction. The residual case is a `Lambda` whose body performs, returned out of the `Handle` and called afterward. The effect axis records that the lambda's *body* performs; the coeffect axis is what has to record that the lambda *value* can perform later. So the rule is a promotion at `Lambda` (the body's unhandled operations become the lambda value's reach of the corresponding handler slots, which is the latent effect on the arrow, Talpin and Jouvelot) plus a check at `Handle` (the result's reach must not contain the handler's slot, else `UnplaceableLease`). Two observations follow. First, this is the first genuine effect-and-lease interaction rule in the system, and the canon says the graded spine "earns the word theorem the day an axis-interaction rule exists in code with a red test that exercises it" (`the-soul-of-vehje-positive-catalogue.md:27`), so this mechanism is where that debt starts being paid. Second, it is intended, not proven; nothing in the current fold does it (`vehje-typecheck/src/lib.rs:262-267` drops the parameter slot and stops there).

**The slot collision is conservative in the right direction.** `slot_of` folds a node index modulo 64 (`vehje-typecheck/src/lib.rs:53-55`, catalogued red test #30 at `lib.rs:419-426`), and both masks are 64 slots (`vehje-ir/src/grade.rs:17`). A collision makes two handlers share a slot, which over-approximates reach, which produces a false refusal and never a false acceptance. The mechanism can therefore be built before the mask is widened, and the widening is an accuracy fix rather than a soundness fix.

**Effect subtraction must be exact per operation.** A `Handle`'s grade is the body's effect minus exactly the operations its clauses handle, joined with the clause bodies' effects. The current fold joins everything and subtracts nothing (`vehje-typecheck/src/lib.rs:299-306`, with the FIXME at `304-305`). Subtracting by mask is unsound when two operations share a slot; the formulation that avoids it entirely is to carry the handled operations as a scope chain down the walk, the way `resolve` already carries binders, and let a lexically handled `Perform` contribute nothing to the mask in the first place. Then the mask is only ever populated by operations that genuinely escape, which are the declared named effects the target's `Permits` set is about.

**Evaluation order must survive the unwind.** Operands evaluate left to right and a host call may have effects (`runtime.zig:337-338`), so an operand that unwinds must do so before any later operand is evaluated. In a carrier-returning tree-walk this holds automatically provided every site that evaluates two children checks the first before evaluating the second. It is violated by any site that evaluates both and then checks. That is a per-site obligation, and it is exactly the kind of thing that is right in the first draft and wrong after the third edit, so it wants a test per multi-child form rather than a comment.

**Handler selection must be a pure function of the program text.** Lexical resolution gives this. A dynamically searched handler would make which clause runs depend on the call path, and the residual would no longer be deterministic, which the design's determinism posture forbids.

**A resuming clause must be refused until the budget machinery exists.** Not silently accepted and not silently ignored. A clause whose body references its resumption is a named refusal now, with the intended behaviour asserted in a test that is catalogued red and flips when CR1 lands. A green test over a handler that cannot actually resume would be a lie about the cost, and the cost is the whole promise.

**What the tree-walk must do, under no allocation.**

`eval` returns a carrier rather than a bare `Value`. In Zig this cannot be an error set, because an error carries no payload and the unwind carries one, so either the return type becomes a union of `Value` and `Unwind { op: u32, payload: Value }`, or `EvalError` gains a sentinel and the unwind travels in an out-parameter. The first is honest and touches every return site in `eval` (`runtime.zig:240-353`); the second is a smaller diff and puts the correctness of about twenty-five sites in a convention rather than in a type. Which is cheaper is a hot-path question on every evaluation step, so it is benched, not asserted; what is *not* negotiable is that the carrier propagates rather than jumping, per section 3's foreclosure note.

Nothing else in the runtime needs to change to be sound. The `Handle` arm compares the carrier's operation against its clause list, applies the selected clause body to the operands, and evaluates it under the handler's own environment rather than the performer's, which is the correct scoping and comes free from passing `cur` unchanged. No frame is unwound because no frame owns memory. The one stale thing to fix in passing is the comment at `runtime.zig:350` claiming `Project` is unimplemented; it was implemented at `runtime.zig:253-276`.

## 6. What I would build first, and what I would refuse to commit to

Build, in one round, the whole of the non-resuming mechanism: the clause region and record with all five fields including the reserved resumption binder; the `Perform` form with its wire tag; the handler scope chain in `resolve` and the perform-to-handler entry in the resolution side-table; the effect scope chain in `check`, which retires the subtraction FIXME and makes `ForbiddenEffect` real; the latent-effect promotion and the escape check, which makes `UnplaceableLease` real; and in the interpreter the carrier return, the `Perform` arm, and the `Handle` arm. Building the representation in halves is what the reserved field exists to make unnecessary, and the passes are each a single arm.

Alongside it, the red tests that are the mechanism's boundary: an operation that escapes its handler inside a returned lambda; a `break` crossing a `continue` handler untouched; a clause whose body references its resumption, refused; two labelled loops whose operations do not collide; an unhandled perform refused at compile time rather than at run time; and the loop iteration bound, asserting the intended unbounded behaviour and left red against the current `EnvFull` at roughly 1024 slots.

Refuse to commit, for now, to five things. The resumption's runtime representation, which is PE2's bench. Whether `Raw` with a host handler collapses into `Perform` with the host as the outermost clause provider, which is a real unification and a real risk to the purity of family operations. Whether the clause region is the same region `Match`'s arms will use, because that is the pattern round's call and deciding it here would decide it without the pattern design. The terminator shape for `Perform` and `Handle` in the block model, which waits on the CFG memo's own open question about whether a block is a linear stream or a tree region (`202607251003_cfg-of-blocks-lowering-design.md:60-67`). And the operand-binding shape beyond currying, which the pattern round may want to replace with clause patterns.

## 7. Open questions

**Does `Raw` with a host handler become a `Perform`?** Frontier B says a host call is a runtime-effect operation the host handles (`the-soul-of-vehje-positive-catalogue.md:23`), which reads as an instruction to unify them, and today `Raw` calls the host directly (`runtime.zig:325-349`). Unifying gives one dispatch discipline and one place where a host failure becomes a performed operation rather than a flat `HostFailed`. Not unifying keeps a pure family operation pure, which matters because arithmetic lowers to `Raw` and an effect on every addition is a grade the whole system then carries. The two readings are both faithful to the canon and they do not both fit.

**Does the effect mask stay 64 slots once operations are `Sym`s?** Under the scope-chain formulation only escaping operations reach the mask, and those are declared named effects, so 64 is generous. Under a mask-subtraction formulation every operation needs an injective slot and 64 is a hard program-wide cap. The choice of formulation and the width are the same decision.

**Is `continue` a per-iteration handler or a structured rewrite?** A handler is uniform with the other four constructs and costs one frame per iteration. A rewrite that folds the rest of the iteration into the else-branch of the `continue`'s condition costs nothing where it applies, and where it does not apply (a `continue` nested inside blocks with statements after it at several levels) it degrades into threading a flag through every subsequent statement, which grows code size and defeats the straight-line block. That leaves two lowerings to keep in agreement. The cost half of this is measurable; the uniformity half is not.

**Does the resumption stay a distinct clause field, or curry with the operands?** A distinct field gives the check a syntactic grip on "does this clause resume" and reserves the multi-shot slot at a known offset. Currying makes the clause record two fields instead of four and makes the resumption an ordinary parameter, which is what it already is under the CPS branch of PE2 and is not under the segmented-capture branch. The answer therefore partly depends on a bench that has not run, and the reserved field is the option that survives either result at the cost of one word.
