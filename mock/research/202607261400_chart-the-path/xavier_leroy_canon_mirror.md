Zig comptime cost is a compile-time property; the harness's per-variant cdylib model measures runtime cells, and `comptime-cost-cliff/findings.md` already states "the runtime bench harness does not apply". What forces it: you cannot dlopen a compile. What the canon would have to say: either the harness grows a compile-time-measurement mode (variants as separate compile invocations, CSV and meta trail preserved), or call 5 gains a stated exception scoped to compile-time cells with the CSV-and-committed-trail half of the mandate kept. A superseding note from op is owed either way; G8 cannot honestly run before it exists.
2. **"No trusted emitter" as a terminal state.** `positive:31`'s heading promises it; `positive:29` lists the emitter seam in the trusted base. Both are honest because the canon marks the closure intended-mechanism-open. The drift risk is in readers, not the text: no differential this project can build removes the seam from the trusted base entirely, it can only shrink what the seam is trusted for (a manifest-hash check reduces it to "the hash function and the bytes hashed"). No superseding round owed; what is owed is that G6 and any successor never claim the seam closed, only narrowed, with the residual named.

## Trusted base and proof obligations

What this roadmap, fully executed as drafted, would actually certify: rustc certifies the well-formedness of the emitted package's shape; Zig comptime certifies the specialisation type-checks against whatever bytes `build.zig` handed it. That second clause is the problem: with no manifest read and no cross-seam differential, nothing certifies those bytes are the package Rust emitted, so the canon's own sentence "the specialisation type-checks against that data" (`positive:29`) is not yet established by any gate; only "against some data" is.

The trusted base at the roadmap's end, honestly enumerated, is larger than `positive:29`'s list, not smaller: the Zig compiler (unpinned until G9 as ordered; pin at G0), rustc, the C ABI marshalling, the byte-image hash (defined, wired by no gate), the emitter seam (open, ungated), the caller-supplied sets (whose replacement mechanism is ungated once the check crate dies), the load verifier's decode (ungated), plus two entries the roadmap adds without naming them: the G5 operation-body language's evaluator at each of its four discharge sites (whose mutual agreement is exactly what a real differential must check), and `build.zig` itself, which becomes a certification-path component the moment G6 lands and which no gate treats as trusted code.

Gates whose stated obligation is weaker than their property: **G6** (self-agreement offered where seam-crossing agreement is the property; the canon killed this exact substitution at `negative:27`); **G8** (numbers without harness, CSV, and pin are not evidence under call 5 and `positive:9`; and its "zero cells" premise is false, the honest premise being the prototype-versus-shipped tag of `positive:7`); **G0** (labelled MECHANICAL while its deletion boundary is an unmade decision about where inclusion refusal and the graded fold live). **G10** is not weaker so much as vacuous as written, since its subject is scheduled for deletion by G0.

## Where the canon is genuinely silent

Each of these is the lead designer's, per the standing gate. The draft already lists five (artifact C's home; whether the mechanism stays comptime; the Arena-tier re-sequencing; `vehje-runtime-driver`; PE2 sequencing). Add these, with the tradeoff stated and no preference:

1. **The per-script proof locus.** `positive:13` says "it proves each discharge statically and then erases it"; `positive:21` says Rust's only binding time is LanguageAuthor and Rust knows no scripts. The canon does not state where a *script's* effect and lease proof is discharged after the correction. The derivable reading is: in the runtime artifact, at Bundler or HostLoader binding time, by machinery Rust extracted as specialisation data. That is a derivation, not canon text; I am one read, and the two-expert rule means a second independent read is owed before G10's relocation target is fixed. The tradeoff: proving in the runtime artifact keeps `positive:45` clean but moves proof machinery into the shipped, no-alloc side; proving at our build (Bundler as part of our packaging) keeps the shipped side dumb but only covers scripts bundled at our build, not host-arriving ones.
2. **The loop encoding.** Loops as recursive bindings plus an exit effect (census) versus a loop Core form. `negative:30` settles that forms are added by algebra, not census; it does not settle whether the algebra demands one here. Tradeoff: the encoding keeps twelve forms and makes G3's tail-call machinery mandatory and load-bearing; a form makes loops trivially bounded but reopens the form set.
3. **The pattern representation** (G13). A Core design round whose trigger the census says has fired. Tradeoff between a structural pattern algebra in Core and patterns as family-level sugar is a Core-shape call, op's by `positive:45`.
4. **The module system** (G15). The canon says nothing. Tradeoff: a load-time module graph puts resolution at HostLoader binding time and interacts with the lattice; a bundle-time concatenation-with-namespaces is simpler and worse, and the current text-concatenation is neither.
5. **Which artifact discharges inclusion refusal** after the split: at our build over the bundled program, at host load over an arriving one, or both, with the refusal diagnostics (`positive:39`) in whichever locus is chosen. The canon states the property, not the locus.
6. **The G8-versus-call-5 harness question** named under unavoidable drift, which is a one-line op call but a call all the same.
