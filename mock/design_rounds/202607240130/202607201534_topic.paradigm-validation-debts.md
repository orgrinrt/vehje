## Preface: the validation and its findings

Before the paradigm hardens into a design document or drives a crate overhaul, it was audited against the
prior-art research and both rounds' paper trail by a Fable-tier reviewer (chosen for direct
embeddable-scripting-VM lineage). The full audit is at
`mock/research/202607201515_metacompile-paradigm-validation/carmack_audit.md`. Read it alongside this topic; the
summary below is not a substitute for it.

The verdict was: sound in its core moves and worth adopting, but not finished thinking, with one unfinished
part load-bearing. The parts that hold are the parts that sit on established theory, and the audit credits them
as restatement rather than invention. Interpretation and transpilation as one output-generation spectrum is the
tagless-final and polymorphic-embedding result (Carette-Kiselyov-Shan; Hofer-Ostermann-Rendel-Moors). Native
demoted from a tier to an ordinary target is what the Futamura projections say: a compiler is a specialized
interpreter, and there is only how much you residualize versus evaluate, no separate category of native.
Runtime generation is the first Futamura projection lifted from the program level to the language level, and the
two-language compile/run split with a small runtime is the QuakeC and Lua mainstream. The value-transfer model
is the best-grounded piece, and the arena-never-moves deletion of the moving-collector handle machinery is the
insight to protect. The real novelty is composition, chiefly the three-axis inclusion-proof unification, not
invention.

Against that, the audit named three debts, which this topic exists to pay.

## The three debts

The first is load-bearing. The paradigm relocated the entire per-script compile pipeline out of the dev-time
Rust compiler and into the shipped runtime, and did not reconcile that with the committed spine. The spine says
the Rust compiler reduces a program to a checked residual, reduces every build-environment effect away, and the
runtime only executes the residual and never implements build-environment semantics. The three-codegen topic
has the composed runtime, per arriving script, run the analyzers, do macro expansion, and do const-eval, which
are build-environment reductions by the spine's own definition. So the runtime now spans both effect
environments, the per-script proofs move from Rust to the runtime, and the effect-derived compile/runtime split
moves from between two artifacts to between two stages of one artifact. None of that was written down.

The second: the lease axis is the weakest load-bearing claim, and its own cited lineage is the evidence against
it. The narrowing that makes it cheap, dropping aliasing because a produced value is immutable, does not hold
for `vehje-lua`'s mutable aliased tables, and Lua is in the census wanting exactly that. And the lineage the
topic cites as endorsement is a cautionary tale: MLKit's pure region inference leaked and ended up needing a
copying collector, and Cyclone shipped a hybrid of regions, unique pointers, refcounting, and a collected heap,
because static regions alone could not carry every real program. The design forbids all three escape valves and
makes inference failure a hard compile error, deferring the annotation ergonomics that the prior art says are
the whole game.

The third: the three axes are lumped as proven the same way, but leases are a different kind of proof. Family
and effect inclusion are decidable set checks that run per language. Soundness of a region-inference algorithm
for all programs of a language is a metatheorem, not a per-language runnable check, so either the lease rules a
language may declare are constrained to a fixed pre-proven schema (with a bound on expressiveness that was never
described) or the uniform "proves the language's disciplines sound" claim is aspirational for that axis.

Under all three sits a meta-critique: the design kept parking the single hardest sub-question of each axis (the
bounded primitive vocabulary, the lease inference failure rate, the no-heap macro expander) behind an
optimistic untested lean, against the workspace's own run-the-experiment rule.

## Debt 1: what the Rust proof actually is, and what it is not

The reconciliation starts from a correction to what "the Rust side proves" means. The Rust dev-time compiler
does not prove anything about a script, because it never sees one. What it proves is about the runtime it
generates: that the generated compiler-plus-interpreter is a correct, sound implementation of the language's
family, effect, and region disciplines, and it holds those proofs as it emits the runtime's Zig or C. The
runtime, so proven, then legitimately performs the per-script compile stage and execute stage, and it carries
the per-script evidence checks the Rust side generated into it. So the spine's "the runtime never does
build-environment semantics" was an artifact of assuming the Rust compiler compiles the program. Correctly, the
runtime does perform build-environment reductions on a script, at its compile stage, and the Rust proof is what
guarantees it performs them correctly. The proof is a proof about the runtime, not about the program.

That resolves the contradiction rather than dissolving it: the runtime doing build-environment work is not a
leak, it is the design, made correct by the generation-time proof. But accepting it commits the design to
consequences, and those consequences are what "perhaps insufficient" points at. They are not flaws in the
framing; they are the work the framing now obliges.

The runtime is a compiler and an interpreter, not a thin evaluator. It carries a parser, the analyzers, a
macro expander, a const-evaluator, and the output-generation targets. "Minimal runtime" is true only in the
distribution sense (no Rust toolchain ships, the footprint is small relative to a general VM), not in the
functional sense; the one-const-`Permits` thin-contract picture from the effect model does not survive as
stated, and the crate taxonomy has to reflect a runtime that contains a compile stage.

Build-environment splits into two build environments. The language's own build-time state (the language
author's registries, manifests, and const inputs, resolved while the Rust side generates the runtime) is not
the same as a script's load-time state (the host's state when a script is loaded and the runtime's compile
stage reduces it). The spine's effect axis conflated them because it only had one compile time. Under the
paradigm there are two: the language build (dev-time, Rust, during runtime generation) and the script build
(load-time, the runtime's compile stage). The effect axis has to name which build environment a `Reads<BuildEnv>`
refers to, or split the marker.

The effect-derived compile/runtime split is now internal to the runtime, between its compile stage and its
execute stage, and that derivation has to be rewritten from "between two artifacts" to "between two stages of
the shipped artifact," with the Rust side proving the split sound and generating both stages.

The lease part of "the Rust proves the runtime correct" is exactly debt 3's metatheorem, and no-heap macro
expansion is debt's hardest instance; both are pulled in here and handled below and in their own reckonings.

The proposed resolution, then: adopt the "Rust proves the runtime, not the script" framing as the reconciliation,
and record the four consequences as the design obligations it creates, rather than leaving the spine and the
paradigm topic contradicting each other in the record. The remaining question this topic should settle with op
is whether that framing is sufficient as stated or whether any of the four consequences reopens the paradigm
rather than merely obliging design work under it.

## Debt 2 and debt 3: the lease reckoning (to settle after debt 1)

Placeholder for the lease axis reckoning: scope the axis to immutable-value consumers with a specified fallback
for mutable ones (Lua), or adopt a Cyclone-style bounded escape valve rather than compile-error-only, and either
constrain the declarable lease rules to a fixed pre-proven schema (naming the expressiveness bound) or restate
the axis's guarantee honestly as weaker than the family and effect meta-checks. Worked in discussion after debt
1 settles.

## The experiments owed (to run before the doc phase)

A tiny general-engine skeleton for the resolve, check, and lease stages over a two-node family, to replace
"logical trace: WORKS" with an executed result. An enumeration of the candidate primitive vocabulary across the
real census families, to test whether it stays bounded or sprawls. A no-heap macro-expansion probe, to test the
hardest instance of the no-alloc constraint. These are small and decide whether the leans hold.
