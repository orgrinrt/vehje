# Topic: the authoring and templating consumers, and the input and emit sides that serve them

**Date:** 2026-07-20
**Phase:** TOPIC (in conversation, decisions recorded as they are made)
**Round:** runtime output + lease proof
**Scope:** the two "other sides" the runtime-performance focus never developed, taken as one topic because they
are the two seams of the same consumer. An authoring or templating consumer (mockspace's columnar-query plus
procedural-document DSL, polka-dots' `.polka`, the typst and scribble register) is, in vehje's terms, a surface
grammar plus a reduce-and-emit target carrying little runtime logic. So its input side (how a surface grammar
plugs in) and its output side (how it emits configured data, documents, or transpiled source) are its defining
seams, and the transpilation end of the output spectrum folds in as one of those emit modes rather than a separate
topic. This topic derives what these consumers need from the framework, across the whole census, not from any one
consumer's shape.
**Reads for current state:** the committed input-side contract (round `202607192358`, topic `202607192300`); the
output-spectrum synth (`202607201618_synth_output-spectrum-interpret-transpile-multitarget.md`); the
mockspace-procedural-docs arc's vehje-canon correction (`202607190650_.../13_vehje_canon.md`); the three-codegens
spectrum (topic 1513); the input-side and grammar-composition threads in topics 2001 and 2055 and the synthesis
`202607210020`.

## Framing: these consumers are the majority, and they are still plugins

The identity recenter established that the representative and largest part of the census leans toward authoring and
templating, whose speed concern is the compile stage and the emit rather than runtime logic. This topic serves
that majority first-class. It also holds the line the mockspace-procedural-docs arc's canon correction drew: the
extension-point contract is derived from vehje's own canon across all its named targets, never shaped around one
consumer's needs, and each authoring or templating language is a plugin on equal footing with `vehje-jomini`,
`vehje-w3`, `vehje-ts4`, and `vehje-lua`, not the core. Serving the authoring majority and refusing to let it
shape the core are both obligations here.

## Current state, and the tension this topic must resolve

### The input side is settled once, then drifted, and the two disagree

The committed input-side contract (topic `202607192300`, 2026-07-19, in a locked round) is decisive and narrow:
the framework's input contract is only that a grammar produces well-formed IR (the shared substrate plus its own
declared family), and the framework owns nothing of the front-end. A grammar consumer brings its own lexer and
parser in whatever technology fits; the framework offers reusable lex and parse building blocks and a node builder
as opt-in libraries, never as a mandatory machine. This is the pandoc-types shape. It explicitly rejected two
alternatives: the framework owning a generic front-end machine parameterised over token kind, and the framework
compiling a grammar-declaration spec into a front-end (grammar-as-data). The grounds were prior art (pandoc,
racket, djot all hand-write each front-end; nobody generates it) and scale (at roughly fifteen forms and thirty
constructors, grammar-as-data machinery costs more than the hand-writing it saves and costs the checkable
exhaustiveness the inclusion check depends on). The one caveat that could flip it was named: if grammars were
authored by non-programmers, grammar-as-data would lower the barrier, but the census is all first-party or
capable-extension-author work.

The later topics drifted the other way. Topic 2001's secondary mappings and topic 2055 proposed a general parser
over a declarative grammar table (attribute grammars plus PEG or GLL), a scannerless PEG emitting a graded IR in
one pass, and Brzozowski-derivative grammar composition by inclusion-not-coverage, with families bringing grammar
fragments that compose, conflict detection at dev time, and the runtime parsing one fixed generated table. The
synthesis `202607210020` carried this forward as the owed input-side story. That is grammar-as-data and a generic
front-end machine, which is precisely what the committed contract rejected.

This is a genuine contradiction in the record, and it is the load-bearing decision on the input side. It is
recorded here as the first thing to settle, not resolved yet.

### The output and transpile side is better settled, and grounded

Output generation is one spectrum from interpretation to transpilation (topic 1513): a target is a point on it,
interpretation reduces everything to a value, transpilation reduces nothing and re-expresses the IR in another
language's source, and every real target is a staging choice between. The output-spectrum synth grounds the
contract: no real Rust compiler ships tagless-final, all use initial encoding (a concrete arena or index tree
walked by a fold or match per target), so the transferable core for no_std and no_alloc is the contracts, not the
traversal machinery: association-list-or-generated-match dispatch, exhaustive-match totality (a compile error on
an unhandled case), inclusion-not-coverage (a target declares the set it supports and refuses the rest by name,
rather than silently dropping), one shared engine written once plus a thin per-target surface linear in the node
vocabulary, and a single typed escape hatch (offer the representations you can be; the target takes what it
understands) for a value the model does not know. A structurally different target forks upstream of the shared IR
and pays an honest fidelity gap (typst's HTML divergence is the worked case).

### What the authoring and templating consumers need from this

Their compile stage is the cheap load-time const-fold-plus-CSE lowering subset (synthesis `202607210020`), which
folds the load-time-known configuration and shares repeated structure at emit, which is exactly where their speed
lives and where load-time bloat is prevented. Their emit is output generation aimed at configured data or
documents (the interpret-toward-a-value or the transpile-to-another-surface ends), never a native tier. The
mockspace-procedural-docs canon correction warns against baking in two of this consumer class's assumptions as if
general: that output is text (a native target is in the canon's own backlog), and that evaluation happens at
generation time in the same process (the runtime is a separate embeddable artifact behind the C ABI).

## First analysis: mirroring the 2026-07-19 Core, content, and effect decisions against the evolved architecture

The Core-forms cut, the effect lattice, the content model, and the twelfth-form dismissal were all settled on
2026-07-19, before the architecture crystallised. They were derived from the ten-consumer census and the
converged prior art, which is exactly a derivation "based on no real tangible concept of the stack or the tech,"
op's words. Since then the architecture evolved a great deal: the graded (co)modal proof spine (binding time a
graded modality, effect a graded monad, lease a reachability coeffect, assurance a grade, one soundness theorem);
certified generation via Zig comptime; the one graded algebraic signature with three projections; reachability
leases; the relational-fixpoint engine; the CFG-of-blocks interpreter with proof-directed strategy selection; and
the recentered identity. The governing principle for this analysis, op's: we change the Core set to fit the tech,
never fit the tech to an old approximation of the Core. Where a 2026-07-19 decision was justified by the census
and the tech now says otherwise, the tech wins.

The verdict, up front: the eleven evaluation forms mostly still fit, because they are about evaluation shape and
the evolution was in the proof, generation, and execution machinery around them, not in the shape of evaluation.
Two decisions mandate change, and one mandates a reframe.

### What still fits (the eleven evaluation forms, with refined governance)

`Lit`, `Var`, `Let`, `Lambda`, `Apply`, `Project`, `If`, `Match`, `Iter`, `Interp`, `Raw` survive as the
eliminator projection of the graded algebraic signature. They name evaluation primitives, and nothing in the
evolution changed what evaluation is. `Match` stays Core for the same reason it was made Core: destructuring does
not desugar to `If` cleanly, and that is a fact about evaluation, not about the tech around it. `No Seq` still
holds (content is a monoid, concatenation suffices). What changed is how the forms are governed, not the forms:

- `Interp` was kept a distinct form because "the staging rule attaches to it." That staging rule is now the graded
  binding-time modality; the form stays, its staging is a grade. The reason for the form is now expressed by the
  spine rather than asserted.
- `If`'s "both arms survive under an emit policy" is now proof-directed strategy selection: a statically-known
  predicate folds so one arm lowers, a dynamic one is a runtime branch at a block terminator. The form is
  unchanged; its lowering is a graded discharge.
- The binder forms (`Let`, `Lambda`, `Iter`, `Match` bodies) now carry the reachability-coeffect lease metadata
  and the `Leased` gate. This adds metadata to existing forms, not a new form, exactly as the panel's doc 03 said.

### Reframe: content-as-values is no longer a deferred separate schema

The 2026-07-19 position conceded content-as-values (the Typst shape: content is a first-class runtime value and a
monoid, initial encoding not tagless-final types), but filed the value-node-kind schema as a deferred separate
follow-up round, with the Doc family as a family beside Core. The evolved architecture subsumes that: the one
graded algebraic signature has three projections, and the introduction (constructor) forms are the produced-value
domain, so a family that adds a constructor gets its value-kind for free. Content-as-values is therefore not a
deferred schema; it is the introduction projection of the signature the whole design now centers on, and the
output-spectrum finding (no real Rust compiler ships tagless-final; all use initial encoding) is the same
conclusion from the emit side. The concession is vindicated and deepened. The reframe: stop treating the value
domain as an owed separate round; it is one projection of the signature, and the Doc family is the document
consumer's introduction fragment. This is a framing correction, not a mechanism change.

### Change 1: the effect lattice splits binding time from effect

The 2026-07-19 effect lattice was `Pure` / `Reads<E>` / `Writes<E>` over a two-member environment axis
(`BuildEnv`, `RuntimeEnv`). That conflated two things the graded spine now separates. `Reads<BuildEnv>` meant both
"reads the build environment" (an effect, a world-change) and "known at build time" (a binding time). The graded
spine makes effect a graded monad (what world-change) and binding time a graded necessity modality (when the
inputs are known), and the debt-reckoning topic already flagged that "build-environment splits into two build
environments." The evolved binding-time axis is not a two-member `BuildEnv`/`RuntimeEnv` chain; it is the
knowledge-source join-semilattice (language-author, bundler, host-loader, runtime, joined, per the panel's H3), so
partial bundling is a join rather than a special case. So the effect lattice as drafted is superseded: the effect
axis becomes a pure graded monad, and the build-versus-runtime staging moves onto the binding-time modality over
the richer knowledge-source lattice. This is a mandated change, and it is a refinement the spine already implies
rather than a reversal.

### Change 2: the twelfth form is re-opened, and the tech now favors it

The 2026-07-19 cut dismissed a twelfth `Handle` form (resumable user-defined algebraic effects) because "none of
the nine census consumers wants this today." The panel's H7 called that the wrong reasoning: algebraic effects
and handlers are the single theory unifying the effect set, the host-call boundary, and macro expansion (an
effect is an operation, a handler services it, a host-call is a host-handled effect, macro expansion is a
compile-stage effect the compile stage handles). The evolved architecture already adopted the signature half of
this: `2001` makes the families the operations of an algebraic-effect theory and names host-calls and macro
expansion as handler instances, then still kept the user-facing `Handle` form deferred as "a later handler, not a
twelfth Core form." So the model is half-adopted and the surface form is the shelved part, and the shelving rested
on the census, which is exactly the reasoning op's principle overrides. Under the tech, the `Handle` form is the
natural completion of a signature that is already an algebraic-effect theory, it is the construction that collapses
three separately-treated mechanisms into one handler discipline, and the user-defined control it enables
(generators, coroutines, backtracking, events) is wanted by the first-party language and by ikiuni's future. This
is the decision op flagged as convincingly proposed and never given attention. It is re-opened here as a genuine
candidate for adoption, not a marked door.

### D6: the framework owns an incremental scaling layer (mechanism only) (settled, op 2026-07-20)

For the authoring/templating majority (and any consumer with a growing arriving-artifact stack), the framework
owns an incremental scaling layer so load times do not bloat as content piles on. It falls out of tech already in
the design rather than being a new bolt-on: the relational engine already evaluates the compile stage semi-naively
(delta-driven), and differential dataflow (Abadi-McSherry-Murray, already cited in 2055) is its incremental-
maintenance extension, so incremental compilation across a stack is differential maintenance of the compile-stage
queries. Fine-grained incrementality (edit one template shard, only that shard re-derives) falls out for free,
because differential dataflow tracks the derivation dependencies inherently, no separate dependency graph. Two
properties come with it: cross-artifact dedup (shared subtrees compile once, keyed by structural hashing, the
identity primitive already in the design; the content-addressed output is persistent-cacheable, so cold-start load
across launches is fast too), and proof-preservation (a cache hit or a deduped subtree is a proven-valid artifact,
because the compiled-away proof rode into its content-addressed output; incrementality does not weaken the
certified-generation guarantee). This is the 2055 engine vindicated again as the incremental-compile brain, whose
largest beneficiary is the authoring/templating majority's edit-one-shard loop.

The boundary keeps the framework from baking in a load model (op's flagged risk): the framework owns the
mechanism (content-addressed keying, cross-artifact dedup, differential recompute, invalidation, over an artifact
set the host declares); the host owns the policy (what to load, composition and override order, the load model)
and the storage backend (memory or disk, host-lent, per the no-baked-I/O discipline from 1845). The framework
never assumes a load order; it incrementally maintains whatever the host declares. Owning the how, not the what.

### D7: the framework is core plus mechanism only; everything on top is ecosystem crates (settled, op 2026-07-20)

The question of what the framework "ships for the authoring/templating majority" was mis-framed on both sides
(earn-your-existence-then-promote, and batteries-included first-party families). Neither is right, because the
framework has no role there at all. The framework provides the core (the Core substrate, the proof axes and their
mechanisms, the output machinery, the runtime ABI, the incremental scaling mechanism, the value transport) and
nothing else. Everything on top, a language's grammar, its families, output families like a Doc family,
utilities, a grammar-as-data tool, a manifestation helper, is an ordinary crate that anyone publishes and anyone
depends on. mockspace can publish its own `vehje-procdocs`, and polka-dots can depend on it and build on top, and
the framework neither knows nor cares. A language spec is regular Rust that builds into a binary; the framework
crate is the heart of it; the rest is the open ecosystem's game.

This means the framework does not provide, promote, defer, track, or privilege any consumer surface, including the
census "first-party" ones (`vehje-jomini`, the first-party language, and so on), which are ecosystem crates on the
same footing as any third-party crate. It sharpens D1: even a first-party grammar-as-data tool is just one more
optional ecosystem crate, not part of the framework's mandate. The framework's scope is exactly core plus
mechanism, and it does not need to know what is built on top.

2. What an authoring or templating consumer is, concretely, in vehje's terms, and how the cheap lowering subset

3. The emit and transpile contract for these consumers: configured data, documents, and transpiled source as

   front-end machine is introduced.

## Decisions

### D1: the framework mandates the IR contract and does not police how in/out are defined (settled, op 2026-07-20)

There is no conflict between the committed `202607192300` contract and the later 2001/2055 direction; they are
different ways to do the same thing. The core is unchanged: one shared IR for every input and every output. The
framework's only mandatory boundary is the in/out IR contract. You choose the inputs and outputs you want to
support, or write your own, and slot them generically into the Rust language-spec dev compiler, all producing or
consuming the one shared IR. The framework does not police how an input or output is defined.

The `202607192300` rejection stands exactly as stated, but only for what it rejected: a MANDATORY, BUILT-IN
generic front-end machine baked into the framework. It did not reject grammar-as-data as an opt-in tool. So
grammar-as-data is legitimate and welcome as opt-in tooling: the framework MAY ship a first-party grammar-as-data
format plus a proc macro (for example `include_str!`-ing a grammar definition) that expands into the generic
IR-producing impls, and anyone may ship their own grammar-as-data tooling. It is a convenience that produces
conforming impls; it is never built in and never mandatory. The symmetric statement holds for outputs (D2).

This supersedes one thing the synthesis `202607210020` carried forward: it named "inclusion-not-coverage extended
to syntax, families bring composable grammar fragments" as an owed FRAMEWORK input-side mechanism. Under D1 that
is not a framework mechanism. Grammar is separate from families; a family is an IR and semantics unit, a grammar
is a front-end that produces IR, and any grammar-composition scheme (Brzozowski conflict detection, composable
fragments, a table parser) is unpoliced opt-in tooling a consumer or a third party may build, not something the
framework owns or couples to families. The "owed input-side grammar-composition topic" from `202607210020` is
therefore reframed: not a framework contract, but at most a first-party opt-in convenience.

### D2: the output side is symmetric (settled, op 2026-07-20)

Same as D1 for outputs: choose the outputs you want, or write your own, slot them generically into the Rust
language-spec dev compiler, all consuming the one shared IR. The framework mandates only the IR-to-output contract
(the target declaration, `Supports`/`Permits`, inclusion-not-coverage, the total per-target surface, the single
typed escape hatch), and does not police how a target is defined. A data-driven or generated target is opt-in
tooling on the same footing as a hand-written one.

### D3: adopt the twelfth Core form, `Handle` (settled, op 2026-07-20)

The `Handle` form (user-defined resumable algebraic-effect handlers, Plotkin-Pretnar) is adopted as the twelfth
Core form, the first change to the ratified Core set, on op's principle that the Core changes to fit the tech.
Consequences, to be worked out fully in the Core-re-derivation topic this spawns:

- Twelve Core forms. `Handle` is a general evaluation primitive (invoke an operation; a handler services it,
  optionally resuming), so it is Core, not a family.
- The effect axis is re-founded on algebraic effects: "effects are operations, discharge is handling" supersedes
  "effects are a static set plus reduction," composing with the effect graded monad as graded algebraic effects (a
  studied combination). Effect inclusion survives, restated: a program's effect is the operations it invokes that
  are not locally handled, and a target `Permits` a set of runtime operations the residual's unhandled operations
  must be included in. Inclusion-not-coverage is unchanged in spirit.
- Host-calls and macro expansion unify as handler instances of the one discipline: a host-call is a runtime-effect
  operation the host handles; macro expansion is a compile-stage-effect operation the compile stage handles. Which
  stage provides the handler is a binding-time coordinate (D4), so the same theory carries the build-versus-runtime
  split the old effect lattice drew by hand.
- Continuations are already in the design: the resumption a handler needs is the one-shot/linear continuation
  machinery `2001` already uses for the streaming value transfer (the defunctionalised one-shot coroutine,
  Bruggeman-Waddell-Dybvig; Berdine et al.). So `Handle` is scoped to one-shot/linear resumption, which is
  no-alloc-tractable and covers generators, coroutines, events, and most async. Multi-shot resumption (full
  backtracking), which needs heap-shaped continuations, is the case to scope or exclude separately, not assumed in.

### D4: split the effect lattice into effect and binding time (mandated, op to veto)

The old `Pure`/`Reads<E>`/`Writes<E>` over a two-member `BuildEnv`/`RuntimeEnv` axis conflated effect (what
world-change) with binding time (when known). The graded spine splits them: effect is a pure graded monad (now the
operations of D3's handler discipline), and build-versus-runtime staging moves onto the binding-time graded
modality over the knowledge-source join-semilattice (language-author, bundler, host-loader, runtime, joined, not a
two-member chain). Mandated by the spine op already blessed; recorded here, worked out in the Core-re-derivation
topic.

### D5: content-as-values is the signature's introduction projection, not a deferred schema (reframe)

Content-as-values (the Typst shape, initial encoding not tagless-final types) stands and is strengthened: it is
the introduction (constructor) projection of the one graded algebraic signature, so a family that adds a
constructor gets its value-kind for free, and the deferred "value-node-kind schema round" is subsumed. The Doc
family is the document consumer's introduction fragment. A framing correction, not a mechanism change.

D3, D4, and D5 together are a re-derivation of the Core substrate from the evolved tech, foundational enough to
outgrow this topic. Spun out to `202607210120_topic.core-re-derivation-twelve-forms-effect-split-handlers.md`,
which holds the mandate and the working-out; this topic keeps the decisions and continues on the
authoring/templating and input/emit thread.
