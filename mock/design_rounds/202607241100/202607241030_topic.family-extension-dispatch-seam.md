# The family-extension dispatch seam

A framework-readiness assessment of whether a consumer (a grammar plus a family plus a target) can attach to the
framework and produce a checked residual found one real framework-level gap. A Core-only consumer attaches
today: the grammar, target, `Checked` mint, and emit all run green end to end. But a consumer whose family
carries payload sub-structure that must be resolved, checked, or folded is blocked, because the three Core
passes treat a `Raw` node as an inert leaf and never descend into its payload.

`Raw { family, payload }` carries the family's own operands as a `NodeList` of child handles into the shared
pool. Those children are ordinary Core nodes (a family operation applied to a `Var`, a `Let`, another `Raw`).
The passes should traverse them exactly as they traverse an `Apply`'s arguments. They do not: `resolve`'s walk,
`check`'s `infer`, and `codegen`'s `fold_core` all match `Raw` as a leaf and return without visiting the
payload. So a `Var` inside a family node is never resolved, its grade is never computed, and it is never folded
into the residual. A consumer cannot inject that traversal without editing framework crates, which makes it the
framework's gap, not the consumer's.

The gap is a documentation-versus-source mismatch as much as a missing behaviour. The `vehje-resolve` DESIGN
and its source doc comment both state the pass is "generic over the family set, with family-extension hooks,"
and the consolidating design topic locks the same shape. The passes are neither generic over the family set nor
do they take a hook. The implementation is behind its own locked design.

## What lands this round

Two things, together the "generic over the family set, with family-extension hooks" the design promises:

1. The base traversal. `resolve` (both `walk` and `walk_record`), `check`'s `infer`, and `codegen`'s
   `fold_core` descend into `Raw.payload`, visiting each child exactly as a variadic Core form's children are
   visited. This alone unblocks a family whose payload is Core sub-structure: the children are resolved,
   graded, and folded.

2. The family-dispatch hook. The three passes become generic over a family-dispatch trait whose default
   behaviour is precisely the base traversal, so a consumer overrides it to add family-specific handling (a
   family binding form, a family operation's declared effect, a family-specific fold) without editing the
   framework. The Core-only path uses the default dispatch, so a program with no family nodes is unchanged. The
   hook is keyed on the `FamilyId` a `Raw` node carries, so a distribution linking several families dispatches
   each to its own handler.

## What stays deferred, honestly

The hook bodies are still the consumer's: what a family node means (how it binds, what it computes, how it
renders) is family-specific and lives in the consumer repo. This round ships the seam the consumer plugs into,
not any family's semantics. The deeper family-specific cases that need the hook to participate in the resolve
scope chain (a family node that introduces a binding visible to its own children in a non-lexical way) are a
documented extension point on the seam, not a blocker for the common case.

## Not in scope

The other deferral-boundary markers stand: the family payload IR shapes (`Match` arms, `Handle` clauses, the
`Raw` payload's own family-interpreted encoding beyond the child-handle traversal), the research-grade eqsat
stratum, the runtime-coupled export, and the open `run` interleave question. This round closes only the
dispatch-seam gap the readiness assessment identified as a genuine framework blocker.
