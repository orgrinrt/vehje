# Topic: the full-arc bench and evidence findings (what the whole round measured and settled)

**Date:** 2026-07-24
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Round:** runtime output + lease proof (open, flat topics, TOPIC phase)
**Scope:** the durable, single-place record of every bench and sketch this long round ran, and what each one
settles for the design. It gathers three bodies of evidence that accreted over the arc: the overnight de-risking
inventory (spikes, sketches, and the in-harness branch and interpreter study, synthesised in `202607211347`); the
interpreter-composition matrix (the carrier cells, rebuilt to the anti-strawman mandate and fairness-audited to
the ISA, `202607221700` and `202607221915`); and the runtime C ABI batched-execute arc (the boundary bench
families and their honest revised conclusion). It states what each finding confirms, what it flips, what it only
sizes, and what it explicitly does not answer, so the consolidation topic and the doc changelist cite measured
numbers where the design currently cites intuition.
**Grounds on:** the evidence synthesis `202607211347`, the composition-matrix build record `202607221915`, the
ABI hole-poke synthesis (`mock/research/202607231735_runtime-abi-batched-execute-bench-design/`), and the design
head after the identity recenter and the twelve-form re-derivation. Chronology governs: where an earlier bench
number was refined by a later, more rigorous re-measure, the later number holds.

## What this topic is, and the honesty it owes

The round converged on a maximal architecture on paper, then ran an unusually large de-risking effort against it.
The effort was not a formality. Several of its findings flip a decision the design had settled by intuition, a few
size a question the design had answered by taste, and a handful come back honestly as "this did not measure what
it was meant to." The value of writing it as a topic rather than leaving it in scattered CSVs and findings files
is that a reader six months out finds one place that says which choice is bench-backed, what the measurement was,
and what its caveat is. It is also the audit surface: a bench that measures the wrong thing is worse than no
bench, and the ABI arc in particular over-claimed on its first pass and had to be corrected under adversarial
review, which is recorded here in full rather than smoothed over.

The whole effort ran on Zig 0.16.0 and Rust nightly-2026-05-28, aarch64 Apple Silicon, through the mockspace
bench harness (each variant compiled as its own cdylib and loaded in subprocess isolation, so no cross-variant LTO
or inlining can blur a comparison; every variant cross-validated byte-exact against an oracle, so a variant that
computes a different answer is rejected, not timed; hardware-counter timing where available). The feasibility
questions ran as standalone sketches with WORKS or FAILS outcomes. The measurement discipline, and its limits, are
stated at the end because for the load-bearing findings the method matters as much as the result.

## Part 1: the de-risking inventory (the findings that change or size the design)

The inventory covered most of the load-bearing feasibility and cost questions the architecture rests on, not just
the two originally-named gating experiments. Its findings split four ways.

### Findings that flip a settled or leaning decision

The node record width: the earlier sketch says 24 bytes with three inline operands beats 16 with two, but the
later carrier re-run makes it a null result, so it is an open measure-then-lock decision, not a settled flip. The
de-risking sketch (`202607211347`) found the 24-byte record (three inline operands) beat the 16-byte record across
every call fraction (3.82 to 4.28 ns versus 4.43 to 5.65 ns), attributing it to avoiding a pool spill on the hot
binop path, with the corpus distribution supporting three operands as the cut (arity at most three covers 96.5% of
the Python-stdlib proxy, a 32-byte four-operand variant tying 24 exactly). The later composition-matrix carrier
run (the PMU re-measure, `.shared/state/vehje-bench-carrier.md`), which is the more rigorous instrument and the
newer measurement, found record width a null result at scale: on the sequential arena stream the density penalty
of the wider record and the pool-spill saving wash out. Newest wins, so the honest status is that record width is
one of the three bench-gated format decisions still open (to lock against a census-specific corpus when real
consumers exist), not a day-one 24-byte flip. What both agree on stands: pool indirection itself is the expensive
part (10.7 ns/node against 4.4 ns/node with inline operands), so the design keeps operands inline; only the exact
inline count is unlocked.

Interpreter dispatch is a plain switch, and it beats tail-threading for vehje. The runtime design had imported
Deegen's tail-call threading ("beats LuaJIT hand-asm by 31%"). The measure contradicts the import for vehje
specifically: a plain switch is fastest across every case (switch 4.58, direct-threaded 6.24, tail 6.38 ns/op; the
op-body-weight sweep confirms switch faster at W=1 through 64). The reason does not generalise from the
dynamic-language literature: vehje's Core is a lean, statically-checked, flat IR with about 25 primitives, so the
switch's branch is well-predicted and the threading machinery costs more than it saves. Tail-threading wins in the
literature only for a hundred-plus-op-kind interpreter, which vehje is not. Consequence: plain switch is the named
dispatch, tail-threading is a measured-and-rejected alternative for this op vocabulary, and `preserve_none` leaves
the critical path entirely.

Native is a ceiling of about 1.0x to 1.2x over a good interpreter, not a 10x keystone. Fairly measured
(L1-resident, non-vectorisable data-dependent index, native arithmetic as an opaque scalar `madd` so LLVM cannot
auto-vectorise the native side to nothing), copy-and-patch native buys about 1.0x to 1.2x over a good register
interpreter across three regimes (DRAM-gather 1.0x, serial-dependency 1.2x, independent-compute 1.2x). The
interpreter's dispatch is already so small that an out-of-order core hides it. The one regime where native wins
big needs auto-vectorisation, which copy-and-patch cannot do: at scale the scalar stencil loses to compiled LLVM
(1.47x at n=4096, 2.58x at 16384) because LLVM if-converts and vectorises where the stencil emits a real
mispredicting branch. So the honest sizing: the interpreter floor is near-native for scalar code, copy-and-patch
is a modest opt-in accelerator (not a dependency, not the keystone), and the transformative 10x-class win is
reserved for a vectorising JIT (LLVM or cranelift) aimed at the rare genuinely compute-bound consumer. This backs
the recenter's position with a number.

The branch strategy is tier-dependent, so one global choice is wrong in one tier. Under interpretation, evaluating
all arms is the worst strategy everywhere (up to 4.0x the field on the whole-program bench), and every one-arm
dispatch (if-chain, jump-table, binary-tree, hot-first) is a statistical dead heat, so the interpreter picks the
simplest jump-table and never eval-all. Under native the ranking reverses where it suits: for a cheap-arm many-way
unpredictable branch, branchless eval-all dominates by 26% over the jump-table baseline (it dodges the mispredict
the dispatch strategies pay); for heavy arms or few-way branches the one-arm strategies tie and eval-all loses.
The tier reversal is the load-bearing result, and it is the measured content of proof-directed strategy selection:
the grade per region selects the evaluator, and the stencil emitter must apply this map rather than a fixed shape,
which is why the "copy-and-patch loses at scale" result is a strategy bug, not a tier verdict.

The comptime kernel must be iterative; it is not a style choice. Recursive comptime SIGSEGVs the Zig compiler at
about 2500 depth and segfaults rather than erroring, a hard wall. The iterative defunctionalised work-stack folds
100k nodes clean. This promotes the defunctionalization owed-piece from "the principled route" to "the only
route," and the same discipline binds the load verifier and the e-graph extractor (recursive extraction hangs on
the cycles congruence closure creates; it must be an iterative cost fixpoint).

### Feasibilities now proven (assumed or hoped, now WORKS)

Each was a load-bearing assumption the design could not proceed without, and each came back WORKS as a sketch: the
certified-generation core (Rust emits validated data, Zig comptime specialises a hand-authored engine, illegal
families unrepresentable, total dispatch, folded inclusion, negatives fail to lower); the dual-locus shared object
(one Zig object linked by a Rust dev host and a C runtime host is bit-identical, so identity holds by construction
and the differential test is defence-in-depth); the reachability binder rule (the N times W naming bound follows
from the binder rule plus a region-promotion bound, the sharper counter-audit fix, not InScope-only); the bounded
multi-shot `Handle` (no-alloc bounded multi-shot via a host-lent budget plus a comptime fit-check plus
deterministic enumeration, overflow failing closed at compile time); the CFG-of-blocks interpreter (straight-line
blocks, control at terminators, bounded frame stack, running loop plus branch plus call correctly); Perceus
exact-meet (in-place-when-unique reuse, collector-free, peak memory equal to the frontier, no runtime refcounts);
the copy-and-patch MAP_JIT probe (allocate, write stencil, patch immediate, W^X toggle, icache invalidate,
execute); the value transport (children-first, no back-patch, zero-copy arena; linear acyclic-by-construction
typed decode; the reserve/commit sink making wire equal in-process); the adversarial load verifier (all five
attack classes rejected fast without crashing, the integer-overflow class defeated by u64 bounds math one width
above the 32-bit index fields); and the C-host panic-free embedding (error codes, no panic crosses the ABI). The
capstone is the strongest single validation that the pieces compose: a 50,000-node script through the full
pipeline (lease inference, cheap lowering, interpretation, value-arena emission, typed validate) runs end to end
in 3.4 ms, with the compile stage (3.24 ms) dominating and the runtime (127 us) cheap, which matches the
authoring and templating majority's compile-heavy, trivial-execute profile exactly, and each stage's per-node cost
matches its standalone bench, so the pieces compose with predictable additive costs.

### Directions confirmed and sized (no change, now with a number)

The relational-fixpoint compile substrate is feasible no-alloc and its feared cost is tamed: the semi-naive reach
fixpoint runs 8M nodes and 16M edges in about 50 ms (630 M-edge-OR/s), and whole-column OR beats delta semi-naive
for the realistic shallow graphs (two rounds, delta bookkeeping not repaid), so the lease fixpoint uses
whole-column. The eqsat explosion is tamed: a proper e-graph saturated with commutativity and distributivity
grows a consistent 5.2x linear (not exponential) in four rounds regardless of depth, because hashcons congruence
deduplicates equivalent forms, which validates eqsat as dev-time-only and the bounded streaming window as a hard
termination-and-memory safety mechanism. The effect proof, the correctness core, is negligible:
thermometer-encoding the grade makes the lattice join a bitwise OR, the graded bind the same OR, and the inclusion
check a subset test, so effect inference runs at 6.14 ns/node and the inclusion gate at 0.39 ns/node (an 8M
program's effect proof discharges in about 3 ms), sharing the exact bitmask-propagation machinery as the
reachability lease fixpoint (one bottom-up DAG join, differing only in payload). The incremental scaling layer
(D6) is decisively validated: a 2000-mod stack cold-loads in 4 ms and warm-loads (edit one mod) in 0.003 ms (one
recompile plus 1999 cache hits), so load cost scales with the change, not the stack size; cold load parallelises
near-linearly (7.7x at 8 threads on the realistic wide-shallow override DAG) with a 6.2 ms serial interner-merge
tail; warm incremental recompile is bimodal and statically knowable (about 1% of the stack for a typical edit, up
to 40% for a foundational base-mod edit). Every Core form's lowering is sized (Match to a jump-table for dense
keys at 0.51 ns; Project to a direct offset at 0.54 ns where static, a self-disabling monomorphic inline cache
where dynamic, with the megamorphic hazard at 1.89 ns; Iter fusion mandatory and pull-based; Resolve on a flat
shadow-stack at 0.91 ns/ref, 6x to 14x over the scope-chain walk; partial evaluation folding block-structured
templates 3.8x to 7.0x). The interpreter value representation is NaN-boxing (8B, 18% over tagged-8B, stores floats
natively), the interpreter is a register VM (1.86x over a stack VM), and record-update reuse is a pure win on both
axes (frontier-bounded peak memory plus 1.5x to 9.7x faster mutation, paid at emit time).

### What the numbers did not settle (recorded, not cited as proof)

The cheap-lowering bench shows no time win because the benefit of const-fold plus CSE is node-count reduction (76%
fewer nodes), not per-op time (LLVM already CSEs the recompute, so the hash-cons lookup is pure overhead in a time
bench); the node-count reduction is the real, design-relevant result. The micro-op transfer-function races are
between operations too cheap for a stable baseline; the findings they support (thermometer encoding makes the
lattice a flat OR, tnum linear ops about 1 ns) stand independently. The one genuinely expensive numeric op is tnum
multiply (149 ns, a 64-bit long-multiplication loop), which needs a known-operand fast path (both-known lowers to
a 0.68 ns concrete multiply). The corpus distributions backing the 24-byte record and the u64 live-binder bitmask
are measured on a Python-stdlib proxy, not the real vehje consumers (which do not exist yet), so a census-specific
corpus measurement is owed when real authoring and templating consumers exist. The copy-and-patch native tier is
proven feasible at the mechanism level but the stencil-extraction toolchain is still deferred, so the per-branch
strategy ranking under a full archetype-JIT is taken to approximate the native tier's rather than measured.

## Part 2: the interpreter-composition matrix (rebuilt anti-strawman, fairness-audited to the ISA)

The de-risking study's dispatch findings were sharp enough to rebuild rigorously. The composition matrix (the
carrier crates under `mock/benches/carrier*`) was built to a five-part mandate: build every proposal (no subset,
no phasing), treat the beyond-runtime approaches as the primary first-class subject, give every composition a
sound and genuinely representative implementation, double-check every dispatch detail down to the ISA lowering so
no composition gets an accidental advantage, and run nothing until an expert panel reviews the built machinery.

The machinery is complete and cross-validated (byte-exact against the switch interpreter or the appropriate oracle
across six designed program profiles and many seeds), and every dispatch cell is confirmed at the ISA level (a
jump table shows its indirect branch, a function-pointer table its surviving indirect call, a tail-threaded
handler its tail branch with zero callee-saved spills, proving the preserve-none mechanism did not silently fall
back to the standard ABI). The dispatch axis over the straight-line IR: switch, function-pointer table,
frequency-ordered and ascending if-chains, balanced bit-tree, preserve-none token threading in three families,
register/accumulator caching, and a per-form null-dispatch floor. The dispatch axis over a real control-flow VM
(loops and branches a straight-line DAG cannot exercise): switch, function-pointer table, and preserve-none token
threading across basic blocks with a loop back-edge as a tail call to an earlier flat index, all unified onto the
shared unchecked operand primitive so the only thing the dispatch axis varies is dispatch. The beyond-runtime
cells, the primary subject: vertical/SoA SIMD interpretation, copy-and-patch aarch64 codegen (real MAP_JIT machine
code, declining rather than miscompiling above the immediate-offset window), trace/superblock dispatch (a guarded
superblock cross-validated on the full result-and-counts triple), and the Zig cross-language cell (switch and
guaranteed-tail-call threading on identical wire bytes, confirmed at the ISA level to be true tail threading). The
value-representation axis (static, runtime-tagged, NaN-boxed), the residual-encoding axis (register/SSA plus
stack-bytecode), and the working-set stages (const-fold with CSE and DCE, superinstruction fusion, liveness slot
allocation, and three sink-emission strategies). The cells deliberately not built are named with their reasons
(perfect-hash degenerates to the function-pointer table on a dense opcode set; interned operands cannot win on a
value-DAG where operand tuples do not repeat), because a non-representative cell is worse than a named absence.

The upstreamable harness features went upstream to mockspace (the cost-model regression fit `total = S + k*I`, the
declarative axis-matrix variant-crate generation replacing the per-bench Python generators, the disassembly
duplicate-check as a fairness guard, the reference-floor ratio column, and the auto duration-floor calibration as
a `timed_calibrated!` macro). Per-stage sub-timing is achieved with no ABI break, via the cost-model's
fixed-versus-marginal decomposition over cumulative-through-stage variants, because breaking the `FfiBenchCall`
ABI would force a migration across every workspace bench variant.

The carrier matrix then ran (the build-complete record predates the runs; a first full run was superseded by a
PMU re-measure). Nine findings, each refining rather than overturning Part 1. Dispatch shape barely matters on
straight-line bytecode: over the flat register IR the dispatch axis (switch, function-pointer table, if-chain,
bit-tree, threaded) is close to a dead heat, which is Part 1's switch-wins read at higher resolution (switch is
the simplest of the near-equals, so it stays the default). Threading wins only on real control flow: the
preserve-none token threading pulls ahead of switch only across basic blocks with loops and branches (the trace
cell runs 0.45x, the one place the threading machinery pays for itself), which is why the design keeps switch as
the straight-line-block dispatch and reserves threading for the CFG terminator transfers where it earns its keep.
Record width is a null result at scale (the finding folded into Part 1: the wider record neither helps nor hurts
once the arena streams sequentially). The register VM beats the stack VM by up to 1.91x (half the instructions at
near-equal per-instruction cost), confirming the register residual encoding. NaN-boxed and runtime-tagged values
beat a static u64 representation at scale (the boxing overhead is hidden and the native-float storage pays off),
confirming NaN-boxing for the runtime value model. Optimiser payoff is program-dependent, and equality saturation
alone can pessimise (it can pick a form that is cheaper by the cost model but worse on the machine), which is why
the lowering objective must be effect-and-machine-aware, not pure rewrite-count. Native runs about 3x on the
crossing-amortisation axis and copy-and-patch equals the hand-written stencil (the two native paths converge, so
neither is privileged). Vertical SIMD is the single biggest win: up to 6x on the first run, corrected to about
4.8x on a symmetric baseline, which is the result that drives the batched-column ABI decision in Part 3 (the
runtime wants a column of records per call so it can vectorise across them). And the native throughput ceiling is
about 1.5x over the good interpreter (slightly above Part 1's 1.0x-to-1.2x scalar figure because the carrier
regimes include cases with more headroom, but the same order: native is a modest multiplier for scalar code, not
a 10x keystone). The composition matrix confirms the de-risking reads and adds the one number that decides the
ABI shape: vertical SIMD is where the throughput lives, and it needs a batched boundary.

## Part 3: the runtime C ABI batched-execute arc (the boundary bench, and its honest correction)

The composition matrix answered "which interpreter shape is fastest." A distinct question spun off it: should the
host-to-runtime C ABI expose a batched-column entry (W records per call) rather than a scalar per-record entry, so
the runtime can vectorise per-record column evaluation across a resident column. The boundary bench families
(`mock/benches/carrier/src/bench/boundary/`) answer it, each crossing into a sibling `carrier-runtime` cdylib
through a resolved function pointer so every crossing is a genuine cross-object call the optimiser cannot inline.

The first-look conclusion overstated the result, and three independent expert reads (measurement, modelling, and
causal-inference lenses) corrected it. The corrections are the honest record: the "W >= 8 threshold" was circular
(the SoA cell runs entirely through its scalar remainder below its hardcoded 8-lane width, so at W below 8 the SoA
cell IS the scalar path; the ABI-relevant knee is W >= 2, any column beats one record); the "2.4x" is
dispatch-amortisation plus partial NEON, a property of how the cell chunks, not of the ABI entry; the decisive
cheap-payload regime was never built on the first pass (every family pinned a heavy 256-node tree-walk, so a ~9 ns
crossing was invisible against a microsecond-per-record payload); and sequential thermal drift (a ~12% monotonic
rise over a long sweep) confounds every cross-W magnitude, so the cross-profile magnitudes are soft and the
qualitative reads plus the cheap-payload families are the load-bearing evidence.

The corrected, honest conclusion, backed by the rebuilt regimes:

The crossing is nearly free: `C_cross` is about 9 ns warm. Its value is negative. It kills "FFI is expensive, so
the ABI must batch to amortise the crossing," and it kills "the ABI must ship per-W monomorphised symbols" (a
runtime-W entry with an internal loop pays essentially nothing over the monomorphised forms at this crossing
cost). The SoA vectorisation win survives the boundary intact and byte-exact: the same 2.3x to 4.1x SoA-over-scalar
throughput holds whether the payload runs in-process or across a real dlopen'd `blr` (measured across residual
sizes at fixed W=64: 3.7x at a 1-node residual, 4.1x at 4, down to 2.3x at 64, holding 2.4x to 2.5x at 256 and
1024). This is the real result and it is what justifies letting the runtime own a resident column and vectorise
it. The payload-cost sweep shows the crossing is negligible against any interpret payload including the cheapest
(the ~2.5 us `null_entry` floor is dominated by the constant seed-fill, not the ~36 ns column crossing; even a
1-node residual dwarfs the crossing ~200:1), so crossing amortisation is a live term only at the native tier. The
native-tier family confirms it: with a tiny 4-node native kernel (the true cheap-payload regime) the batched entry
amortises the crossing about 3x, `native_ffi_w` falling from 34.7 us at W=1 (256 crossings) to 11.6 us at W=256
(one crossing), converging onto the 11.4 us in-process floor; a per-record entry pays about 23 us of crossing over
the column (about 66% of its time), which the batched entry removes. The cold regime confirms the warm crossing
was a lower bound: with a seed-data-dependent index over sixteen distinct call targets (so the indirect branch
genuinely mispredicts) and a matched warm baseline, a mispredicted crossing costs about 6x a predicted one at W=1
(cold_null 33.5 us versus warm_null 5.5 us), the penalty falling off as W grows and gone by W >= 16.

The decision, evidence-complete: expose one runtime-W batched column entry taking W >= 2. It is justified at every
tier for complementary reasons: vectorisation everywhere (2.3x to 4.1x SoA, surviving the boundary byte-exact),
plus crossing amortisation once the payload is cheap enough (the native and copy-and-patch endgame). No per-W
symbol zoo, no amortisation cleverness beyond a column entry, because the crossing itself is free at every
interpret tier and the runtime, not the ABI, owns the SIMD width. Raw data under
`mock/benches/.bench_history/abi_*.tsv`; the cross-profile full run is confirmatory of these reads.

## The measurement discipline and its limits (the audit surface)

Because several findings are load-bearing, the method is stated so it can be checked. Every strategy compiled as
its own cdylib in subprocess isolation, so no cross-variant LTO blurs a comparison; every strategy cross-validated
byte-identical, so a divergent computation is rejected not timed; the boundary crossings are genuine cross-object
`blr` through resolved function pointers, so the optimiser cannot inline the crossing away. The known LLVM defeats
were handled explicitly: branch arms `#[inline(never)]` so a real branch stays a real branch (LLVM if-converts a
cheap branch to branchless csel otherwise, erasing the very mispredict the bench measures); `black_box` defeats the
copy elision and the ICF that would make a memcpy or a duplicate handler vanish; an opaque scalar `madd` on a
data-dependent index defeats the auto-vectorisation that would collapse the naive native baseline to near-zero,
which is the whole reason the native ceiling is 1.2x and not a misleading 10x. The honest limits: the harness caps
working sets at 16384 bytes, so cache-bound-past-L1 effects are out of scope and live as standalone measurements;
the interp tier is a real interpreter over a mockup IR, not the eventual vehje runtime, so its absolute numbers
are representative-not-final and the strategy rankings are the transferable part; the branch archetypes and the
proxy corpus are directional, chosen to map the strategy space rather than predict a specific consumer's mix; and
the ABI cross-profile magnitudes carry thermal drift, so the qualitative reads and the cheap-payload families
carry the conclusion.

## What the whole arc settles for the design (the corrections that land)

In priority order, the corrections the consolidation topic and the doc changelist must encode. The wire format:
operands stay inline (pool indirection is the measured expensive part), but the exact inline count is an open
measure-then-lock decision, the earlier 24-byte-three-operand flip having been washed out to a null result by the
carrier re-run, so the format commits to inline operands and a width to be locked against a real census corpus,
not to 24 bytes day-one. The runtime dispatch: plain switch is the named straight-line-block dispatch, threading
reserved for the CFG terminator transfers where it wins, tail-call threading otherwise a measured-and-rejected
alternative for vehje's op vocabulary, `preserve_none` off the critical path. The native
tier: the measured 1.0x-to-1.5x ceiling recorded, copy-and-patch an opt-in accelerator whose stencil emitter
applies the tier-dependent branch map, the vectorising JIT the only path to the 10x class. The comptime kernel,
load verifier, and e-graph extractor are all iterative work-stacks, not recursive, because recursion is a hard
compiler wall. The branch-strategy map is the content of proof-directed strategy selection, cited where the design
says the grade per region selects the evaluator. The runtime C ABI exposes one runtime-W batched column entry (W
>= 2), no per-W symbol zoo, no crossing-amortisation cleverness. And the confirmations to cite rather than
re-derive: the relational substrate cost (about 50 ms at 8M), the eqsat linear-5.2x growth bound, the effect
thermometer cost (about 3 ms at 8M), the incremental scaling numbers (4 ms cold, 0.003 ms warm, 7.7x parallel) as
the concrete answer to the load-bloat concern, and the capstone 3.4 ms end-to-end as the evidence the pieces
compose. The honest non-results (cheap-lowering is node-count not time, the proxy corpus, the deferred stencil
toolchain) are named where the relevant decisions cite their support, so a future reader does not over-read a
proxy or a swamped bench.
