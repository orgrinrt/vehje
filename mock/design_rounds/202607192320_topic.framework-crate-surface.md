# Topic: the framework crate surface

**Date:** 2026-07-19
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** What crates the framework is, given the settled contracts, and what moves out to consumer repos.
Continues `202607192254_topic.framework-identity.md`, `202607192300_topic.input-side-contract.md`,
`202607192310_topic.output-side-contract.md`.

## The reshape, in one sentence

The current crates are a linear single-language compiler pipeline (`vehje-ir` to `vehje-lex` to `vehje-syntax`
to `vehje-resolve` to `vehje-typecheck` to `vehje-schedule` to `vehje-codegen`, plus the runtime pair, all "for
the Vehje authoring language"). Under the framework identity they split into three tiers: framework crates the
consumers must have, opt-in infrastructure crates a consumer may reuse, and consumer repos that bring the
front-end, family, and targets.

## The organizing pattern

The input-side decision set the pattern: the framework **mandates the contracts and offers the
infrastructure**. It mandates the IR contract (produce well-formed substrate-plus-family IR) and the output
contract (a target folds the IR, declares its `Supports`/`Permits` sets, is inclusion- and effect-checked). It
offers the front-end as opt-in, never mandated. The rest of the surface follows the same pattern.

## Framework crates (mandated, this repo)

- **`vehje-ir`**, the shared representation, the standalone pandoc-types-style crate everything depends on: the
  Core substrate forms (arena, `u32`-indexed initial encoding, variable-arity children via a
  `cranelift-entity`-style `EntityList`/`ListPool`), the `Family` extension trait plus effect classification,
  the family-set and effect-set typestate (reusing `hilavitkutin-api`'s `AccessSet`/`Contains`/`ContainsAll`/
  `Concat` with vehje family and effect marker types as members), the node builder (monoid smart-constructors),
  and interner integration (`hilavitkutin-str::Str` plus the generation-time arena). Deps: notko, arvo,
  hilavitkutin-api, hilavitkutin-str.
- **`vehje-codegen`**, the output machinery: the one generic fold, the `Target` trait (compile-time, `type
  Supports` family set + `type Permits` effect set as associated type-level sets), the inclusion-check plus
  effect-check plus `Checked<T>` gating, and compile-time target composition (a distribution links its targets,
  a generated match resolves a target name to a monomorphised emit, no `dyn`). Reshaped from the shipped `dyn`
  registry. Deps: ir, notko, hilavitkutin-api.
- **`vehje-schedule`**, the pass DAG over `arvo-graph` for multi-pass consumers. Loses its current dependency on
  typecheck. Deps: ir, arvo-graph, arvo-bitmask.
- **`vehje-resolve`** and **`vehje-check`** (the reshaped `vehje-typecheck`), the Core-level semantic passes:
  resolve, check, and interpret bounded on the shared Core, generic over the family set, with families
  extending them for their own forms. These are framework-owned, not offered, because the shared passes are
  what makes the shared IR real rather than nominal (see the decision below). The common target-agnostic work
  (name resolution over Core binders, the borrowed non-allocating scope chain) lives here once; family-specific
  semantics (coherence, routing, fragment resolution) are family extensions, not separate consumer passes. Deps:
  ir, arvo, hilavitkutin-api, hilavitkutin-str.
- **`vehje-runtime-abi`**, the C ABI: the `Checked` residual (effects proven within `{Pure, RuntimeEnv}`)
  lowered to the runtime representation. Deps: ir, arvo.
- **`vehje-runtime-driver`**, the Zig runtime embedding and compiler-side dispatch over the ABI. Deps: ir,
  runtime-abi, hilavitkutin-api.
- **`vehje`**, the two contracts' public API (the `Grammar` plug-in trait: produce IR; the `Target` plug-in
  re-export), orchestration, and the distribution-composition entry point. A reference distribution lives here.
  Deps: ir, codegen, schedule, runtime-driver.

## Offered infrastructure (opt-in, this repo)

- **Front-end scaffolding** (the current `vehje-lex`/`vehje-syntax`, reshaped generic, or folded into a
  `vehje-frontend`). A consumer may reuse it or bring its own front-end. Not mandated (input-side decision).

## Consumer repos (separate, not this repo)

The first-party general-purpose language (name TBD), `vehje-jomini`, `vehje-w3`, `vehje-ts4`, `vehje-lua`, the
native target, polka, ikiuni-script, viola-lints, mockspace-docs. Each brings a grammar (front-end producing
IR), a family (node kinds plus a `Family` impl plus effect classification), any family-specific semantic
checks, and one or more targets (a `Target` impl declaring `Supports`/`Permits` plus its lowering). The current
`vehje-lex`/`syntax`/`resolve`/`typecheck` "for the Vehje authoring language" framing, and the current `vehje`
CLI's language orientation, move to the first-party-language consumer; what stays here is the framework and its
reference distribution.

## Decided: framework owns the Core passes, families extend

The Core-level semantic passes (resolve/check/interpret bounded on the shared Core) are framework-owned, with
families extending them. This is settled by `05_correction_and_aim.md`'s strongest surviving technical finding:
the shared IR is only real, not nominal, when the passes are shared. "`Ir<V: Vocabulary>` alone gives one
generic type, not one shared vocabulary; passes over `Ir<VehjeVocab>` do not run over `Ir<QueryVocab>`. The
value depends on a fixed expression core that both front ends lower into, with resolve, check and interpret
bounded on that core and per-language families extending it." Fully consumer-owned analysis makes the IR
nominal; merely offering the passes risks the same the moment a consumer skips them.

The heterogeneity concern (coherence vs routing vs fragment resolution) is answered by the extension model, not
by mandating one monolithic shape: family-specific semantics are family extensions bolted onto the shared Core
passes, and the passes are generic over the family set (trait-based extension), composing with the typestate
spine like the family and effect axes. The dividing line against the input-side "offer, don't mandate" decision
is sharp: anything varying with surface syntax is per-consumer (the front-end, offered); anything operating on
the shared Core is framework-owned (the passes). Lexing Clausewitz braces versus Lua is irreconcilable surface
work; resolving a Core binder or walking a Core scope chain is the same work for every consumer.

The compiler passes stay single-shot DAG walks scheduled by `vehje-schedule` over `arvo-graph`, not
hilavitkutin WorkUnits; only the Zig runtime uses hilavitkutin's morsel-driven execution. The compiler/runtime
seam holds here as it does for dispatch and effects.

## What transfers, what drops (the current mock crates)

Almost all of the current mock crate code drops. Every crate's own doc-comment says "for the Vehje authoring
language", which is the category error the reframing invalidates at the root: the code was built assuming vehje
is one language, so it inherits the wrong shape wholesale, not in fixable spots.

- **Drop entirely** (consumer code or superseded shape): `vehje-lex` (1032 lines) and `vehje-syntax` (518) are
  the first-party language's front-end, built to the 9-kind `Ast`; under "framework owns no front-end" they
  leave the framework repo. `vehje-resolve` (558), `vehje-typecheck` (592), `vehje-codegen` (473) are the old
  single-language resolver/validator/codegen over that `Ast` with the `dyn` registry, all superseded by the
  Core-pass-plus-family-extension and compile-time-dispatch shape. The `vehje` CLI and `vehje-runtime-tests`
  are superseded.
- **Mostly drop, tiny salvage**: `vehje-ir` (374) loses the 9-kind `AstNodeKind` and Rust-shaped `TokenKind`
  (superseded by the 11 Core forms; tokens are front-end); only generic infra (`Span`, `FileId`, `ByteOffset`,
  `Diagnostic`) might survive, re-evaluated against the arena/interner/family/effect design.
- **Reshape** (framework-shaped, design pending): `vehje-schedule` (51, generic arvo-graph scheduler, likely
  largely survives), `vehje-runtime-abi` (269) and `vehje-runtime-driver` (195) reshape once the C ABI is
  settled.

Net: roughly 3800 of about 4100 lines drop or get rewritten; maybe 250 lines of generic infra survive or
lightly adapt. The clean path is to drop the old single-language pipeline wholesale and author the framework
crate surface fresh; the old code is git-history audit trail, not foundation. Mechanical execution is this
round's src CL (delete old, author new) in IMPL phase. This is also why the ~72 arvo violations tracked to
#207 were correctly not fixed: they live in `vehje-lex`/`vehje-syntax`, the crates that leave the repo.
