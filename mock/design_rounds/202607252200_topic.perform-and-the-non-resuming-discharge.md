# Topic: Perform, and the non-resuming discharge

**Date:** 2026-07-25
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** `vehje-ir` (a Core form and a clause region), `vehje-resolve`, `vehje-typecheck`, `vehje-runtime-abi` (the wire), `runtime-zig`.
**Oracle:** the canon (frontier B and C), the completeness census, and `mock/research/202607252100_handle-clause-representation.md`.

The census found that the Core has no evaluation form for `return`, `break`, `break v`, `continue`, `?`,
`loop`, or `while`, and concluded that `Handle` already covers them through its cheapest discharge, the one
that never resumes. That conclusion holds. A neutral expert read commissioned against the same question
sharpened it in three places, corrected it in one that matters, and found an operational blocker neither the
census nor this agent had seen. This topic records the settled shape and is explicit about which parts came
from being corrected, because a design record that hides where it was wrong is less useful to the next reader.

## The finding that reframes the round

`Handle` is an eliminator with no matching introduction. Nothing among the twelve forms builds a term that
*performs* an operation, so the handler has nothing to handle. That is not a census gap, which the negative
catalogue already ruled out as the wrong way to grow the Core; it is an incompleteness in the algebra. In
Plotkin and Pretnar the free model is generated by the operation symbols and the handler is the homomorphism
out of it, so shipping the homomorphism alone is half an algebra.

That matters because it decides the register of the argument. The Core gains a thirteenth form here on exactly
the ground that admitted the twelfth: by algebra, not by census. `Perform { op: Sym, args: NodeList }`.

## Where the earlier reasoning was wrong

This agent's own derivation had concluded that `Raw` should perform, with handler lookup preceding the host
call, reading the canon's "a host-call is a runtime-effect operation the host handles" as licence to make the
host the outermost handler. The objection that overturns it is not the performance one this agent had already
noted, and it is decisive: **arithmetic lowers to `Raw`, and arithmetic must not acquire an effect.** An effect
on `1 + 2` is a grade the handler discipline then has to subtract everywhere, which is the effect-system tax
the design cannot afford. The rejection is on typing, not on ergonomics, and it is the difference between a
Core that stays cheap for programs that use none of this and one that does not.

A second correction, smaller and worth keeping. The derivation had reserved room for evidence passing as the
optimisation that would later turn a handler search into a direct index. That is the right mechanism for the
wrong problem: Koka's evidence-passing translation exists to make a *dynamically* scoped handler search into a
known jump, and resolution here is lexical, so there is no search to eliminate. Static binding is also the safe
choice rather than merely the fast one, which is the tunnelling argument of Zhang and Myers: it is what stops a
caller's operation being caught by a callee's handler.

## The shape

**A clause carries five fields**: the operation identity, a resumption binder, an operand arity, the handler
body, and a span. It lives in a fourth caller-lent region alongside the three the IR already has, not in the
child pool and not as a node variant. The pool holds node references, so fitting an operation identity into it
is a pun; and a non-evaluating node in the term arena would make four passes that currently walk a clause list
as terms each acquire a skip rule. The cost is honest and worth naming: the host sizes and lends one more
buffer, which is the fourth instance of a cost already paid three times.

Operands curry through the body's lambda chain rather than taking binder slots on the clause, which reuses
machinery that already works end to end in resolve, check, ANF, and the interpreter. The resumption does not
curry, because the check needs a syntactic grip on whether a clause resumes.

**The operation identity is a freshly minted `Sym`, not a small dense id.** The reason is labelled `break`. If
operations came from a fixed vocabulary, two loops in one function would share the `break` operation and the
inner handler would catch the outer's exit. Minting a fresh operation per handler site makes labelled break and
labelled continue fall out with no additional mechanism, and an unlabelled `break` binds to the innermost
enclosing handler because that is the first frame whose clause list matches. The generator, the free domain
tags, and the structural disjointness from source names all already exist and are tested.

**The discharge is not a clause field.** The node comment lists it as one. Under frontier B the discharge is
which stage provides the handler, which is the binding-time coordinate the grade already owns, so authoring it
on the clause would author a derived quantity. Two things are authored; the third is a grade.

## Why the non-resuming half separates, and what that costs

The unwinding fragment needs no continuation object. Three properties that already hold make it free:
evaluation is strict and left to right, so what abandoned frames were going to do has either happened or is
being abandoned deliberately; nothing runs on the way out, because there is no `finally` clause in the design;
and nothing is reclaimed on the way out, because both arenas are bump regions that never pop. The discipline
that looked like a cost is what makes the unwind free.

The resuming half needs a representation of the rest of the computation, and that is an open bench-decided fork
op ordered run. Coupling them puts the widest remaining language gap behind a bench about continuation capture
that changes nothing about the clause record.

The separation costs exactly one reserved word: the resumption binder ships now and stays unbound, because
adding it later widens every clause record and breaks every image. A clause whose body references its
resumption is a named refusal, with a catalogued red test asserting the intended behaviour that flips when the
bounded-multi-shot work lands. A green test over a handler that cannot actually resume would be a lie about the
cost, and the cost is the whole promise.

**One property must be preserved from the first landing.** If a later round adds a `finally` or a
resource-release clause, the unwind must run code at each frame it passes. A propagated carrier can do that; a
direct jump to the handler cannot. So the implementation is a carrier that propagates, and that is a
correctness constraint on the future rather than a preference today.

## The blocker: this round does not make loops usable

The loop encodings need no new form, and the census was right about that. What the census did not say is that
they are correct denotationally and unusable operationally, for two reasons that are quantifiable and were
verified against the source.

The environment arena bumps and never pops, by design, so that a captured environment stays readable for the
whole run. The lent region is 1024 slots. A loop body with `k` bindings burns at least `k + 1` slots per
iteration, so a `while` terminates with an exhausted-arena refusal after roughly `1024 / (k + 1)` iterations,
which for a three-binding body is a few hundred. Separately, the interpreter evaluates a closure body by native
recursion that is not in tail position, so a self-recursive loop grows the native stack per iteration.

Neither is a reason to defer this round, and both are a reason not to claim it delivers working loops. Early
exit is correct and useful the day this lands: `return` from a function, `?` on an error branch, `break` out of
a bounded loop. Unbounded iteration waits on a trampoline and on reclaiming an iteration's bindings, and the
loop-iteration bound gets a red test asserting the intended unbounded behaviour rather than the current limit.

Reclaiming an iteration's bindings requires proving nothing captured them, which is escape, which is what the
reach mask computes. Tonight's other sketch found that reach does *not* give the reuse analysis its uniqueness
verdict, because uniqueness is last use and reach is escape. The same finding says reach is exactly right here:
reclamation asks whether anything escaped, which is the question reach answers. One analysis, two consumers, and
knowing which consumer gets which half is the useful part.

## What this round settles, and what it deliberately does not

It settles the clause record, the perform form, lexical resolution through a handler scope chain, the effect
scope chain that retires the subtraction FIXME, the latent-effect promotion and escape check that make two
declared-but-never-constructed error variants real, and the interpreter's carrier.

It does not settle the resumption's runtime representation, which is the standing bench. It does not settle
whether a `Raw` served by the host collapses into a `Perform` with the host as the outermost clause provider:
frontier B reads as an instruction to unify them, and keeping a pure family operation pure argues against, and
those two readings are both faithful to the canon and do not both fit. That one is surfaced rather than
decided. It does not decide whether the clause region is the region `Match`'s arms will use, because that is
the pattern round's call and deciding it here would decide it without the pattern design.
