# Topic: the Carmack faults are design work, not a demotion; the maximal shape is the target

**Date:** 2026-07-20
**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** vehje certified-generation + runtime-output arc, post-Carmack
**Source topics:** `202607202001_topic.converged-architecture-shape.md`,
`202607202055_topic.attack-meta-attack-adjudication-and-the-grounded-stack.md`
**Source research:** `mock/research/202607202122_carmack_reaction_grounded-stack.md`

Carmack read the converged shape (2001) and the grounded stack (2055) against vehje's real constraints and
returned a verdict of the form "the floor is a buildable v1; re-tier the unification layer off the critical
path and build the floor first." That verdict is rejected here, at the frame. Its concrete faults are not; they
are real and they are the agenda. This topic separates the two, records op's position, and sets the debate that
follows.

The distinction is the whole point. Carmack found genuine holes: the single relational engine as described
collides with our own no_alloc gates and the dual-locus reality; the grounded stack contradicts itself on
whether load verification is a fixpoint query or a linear decode; copy-and-patch is runtime code generation as
far as the OS is concerned and no document names W^X; the shared-node region tag cannot be written at emission
time without a table indirection; the reachability bitmask has an unspecified overflow cliff; the interpreter
hot loop and the diagnostics surface are undesigned; the stencil toolchain and the dual-locus authoring story
are unbudgeted. Every one of those is a hole in the design. None of them is an argument for building something
smaller. A hole in a design is filled by finishing the design, not by choosing a design with fewer holes because
it is easier to reach today.

## The re-tiering is YAGNI in sheep's clothing, and we reject it

Carmack's remedy for each hard piece is the same shape: take the ambitious unified mechanism, move it to a
"bench-gated research track" or a "later north star," and put a simpler local mechanism on the critical path so
the floor ships. The one relational engine becomes three small analyses sharing bitmask primitives. The
equality-saturation compile stage becomes hash-consing plus a specializer, with the e-graph told to come back
once it beats the simple pipeline. The assurance-indexed logical relation becomes the 1845 skeleton, with the
unified relation held for a pass "a later effort may fund." AARA becomes three constants. The provenance
semiring is held "with a light grip."

That is the exact reasoning this workspace exists to reject. It is "you are not going to need the unified thing
yet, so do not build it yet." The justification is always plausible (it is more work, it is unbuilt, it is
Iris-scale, a simpler thing does the same job today) and it is always the defer instinct producing a
locally-cheaper artifact that forecloses the thing we actually set out to build. We do not ship the floor and
promise ourselves the unification later. We design the unification now, completely, and only then does anyone
touch code. The two prior halves of this same instinct, my relay of the re-tier and Carmack's re-tier itself,
are both named here so no future reader re-trusts either as a settled call.

The maximal shape is not ambition for its own sake. It is the reason vehje is written in a language whose type
system is the verification layer, on a stack whose whole thesis is that the substrate is designed to the bottom
before it is used. A framework whose identity is "one graded provenance-semiring judgment, one relational engine
under every analysis, one logical relation certifying the whole generation" is worth exactly as much as its
willingness to design that one-ness through the hard parts. The moment we accept "three analyses that happen to
share a bitmask" because the single engine is hard to build no_alloc across two loci, we have shipped a
different, smaller framework and told ourselves it was staging.

## The genuine faults, recast as open design problems

Each of these is a problem to solve inside the maximal shape. The framing for the debate is "design this so it
holds," never "is this worth keeping."

1. **The no_alloc, dual-locus relational engine.** The grounded stack put "one oatlog-shaped semi-naive
   relational engine, with lease inference, eqsat lowering, and load verification as queries on it" at the base.
   Carmack's break: oatlog is a Rust proc macro emitting heap-using Rust; the compile crates are lint-gated
   no_std + no_alloc; and per 1513 the per-script analyses for arriving scripts run inside the composed Zig
   runtime, where a Rust proc macro does not reach. So "oatlog-shaped" cannot mean "use oatlog." It means design
   our own AOT relational-engine generator that emits no_alloc code into both loci: the Rust dev-time compiler
   and the generated Zig runtime. Carmack calls that "a full compiler project standing under the compile stage"
   and treats the size as disqualifying. It is not disqualifying. It is the work. The open problem: specify that
   generator. What is its input schema (the relations, the rules)? What no_alloc data structures back the
   semi-naive frontier (fixed-capacity worklists, arena-backed relation tables sized by the three structural
   bounds)? What does it emit into the Rust locus, and what into the Zig locus, and how is the emitted engine
   the same engine (differentially tested, or generated once from one description into both)? This is the
   central design problem of the whole arc and it must be answered in full.

2. **Load verification: fixpoint query or linear decode.** 2055's A6 resolution re-derived the load check as a
   typed structural decode, complete and linear. The grounded-stack summary two sections later still lists load
   verification as one of three queries on the monotone fixpoint engine. These cannot both stand as written. The
   open problem: make the load path coherent. Is the structural decode a degenerate zero-iteration case of the
   relational engine's evaluation (so the unification holds and the "query" framing survives as a special case),
   or is it a genuinely separate linear mechanism the engine does not touch (so the engine's query list loses an
   entry and the unification narrows)? The design must state which, and if the answer narrows the engine's remit,
   it must say what remains on the engine and re-justify the engine on that remit, rather than quietly dropping
   the engine because its query list shrank.

3. **The native tier as runtime code generation.** Copy-and-patch writes bytes and then executes them. W^X
   applies; macOS needs MAP_JIT plus the write-protect toggle; iOS forbids it without an entitlement third
   parties do not get; consoles forbid it; hardened hosts disable executable mapping wholesale. "No compiler at
   the embed site" is satisfied; "no JIT permissions at the embed site" is not, and the second is the one that
   bites on the platforms a game-embedded runtime targets. This is not a reason to demote copy-and-patch. It is
   a reason to design the native tier's platform-capability model in full: which targets get stencils, what the
   runtime does when executable mapping is denied at load time (a tested tier-down path, not an assumption),
   whether the tier is a build-time or run-time choice per composed runtime, and how the tier-down interacts
   with the certified-generation guarantee (the residual is the same; only the execution strategy changes). The
   interpreter being present as a floor everywhere is part of this design, not a replacement for it.

4. **The stencil extraction toolchain, budgeted.** Extracting stencils requires compiling each operation with a
   continuation-passing calling convention (clang guaranteed-tail-call plus preserve-none), parsing object
   files, and pulling relocation records into patch tables. Zig ships clang, so the toolchain is present at our
   build. Carmack files this as "weeks of grubby object-format work in the TCB," unbudgeted. The open problem:
   design it and budget it. What is the stencil description, what is the extraction pass, what are the per-target
   ABI edge cases, how is the patch-table format specified, and where does it sit in the trusted computing base?
   Unbudgeted is a design gap; the answer is the budget, produced by designing the thing.

5. **The interpreter hot loop.** The arc spent thousands of lines on the proof spine and near-zero on the loop
   that runs every frame in the flagship consumer. The open problem, at the weight the lease axis got: dispatch
   shape (Zig `@call(.always_tail)` threaded dispatch versus switch), value-node operand packing against the
   fixed-width-record-plus-child-index-pool arena (small-operand inlining is a wire-format decision, so it
   couples to the arena layout that must be decided anyway), and the cache behavior of the arena walk. This is
   where vehje's per-frame performance identity is decided, before any native tier exists, and it is owed a full
   topic.

6. **Diagnostics and provenance through the analyses.** Strict-by-default with lease-inference failure as a
   compile error means modders meet lease errors as first contact. A bitmask tells you inclusion failed; it does
   not tell you which binder, bound where, escaped through what. The open problem: design spans and binder
   provenance carried through the fused walk (and through the relational engine's derivations) from the start,
   because retrofitting provenance onto a bitmask analysis is miserable. Error reporting is a product surface the
   maximal design owes, not an afterthought.

7. **The dual-locus authoring story for every analysis.** Family, effect, and lease checks run in Rust at dev
   time for bundled scripts and inside the composed Zig runtime for arriving scripts. The open problem: are the
   general algorithms written twice and differentially tested, or written once in a form both loci consume
   (generated from one description, or a shared no_alloc core compiled into both)? This is the same class of
   problem as the point-3 handoff and it interacts directly with problem 1 (the engine generator is one obvious
   "written once, emitted into both" answer). The design must state the mechanism, not leave it implied.

8. **Wire-format details that must be fixed on day one.** The shared-node region tag cannot be written into the
   node record at emission without a region-table indirection (nodes carry a region id, the table finalises at
   chunk close, no byte is touched twice); the reachability bitmask needs a stated overflow policy (hard error
   versus demotion to the generational residual, which changes the width you pick and the failure mode modders
   see); the arena needs a decided alignment-and-stride layout for the typed structural decode to stay
   branch-predictable and SIMD-friendly. These are small designs, but they are load-bearing and they belong in
   the format now.

## What Carmack got right that does not force any demotion

The floor pieces he endorses are genuinely sound and stay: the three-layer artifact model and the three named
codegens (1513); the value-arena and the reserve/commit sink (1315); the graded lease axis reducing to bitmask
joins with a depth-lease floor and a per-reference generational residual (1316, point 5); Perceus-style
emission-time finalisation on shared roots; the typed structural decode; the three named structural bounds; the
two gating experiments and their sequencing; differential testing from one semantic definition. The debate trail
also earned four real mechanism kills (shared-implies-promoted, PE-as-extraction, tnum-for-structure, the
depth-cap pun); those stay killed.

None of that endorsement is a reason to demote the unified layer. "The floor is sound" and "the floor is
sufficient" are different claims. Carmack proved the first and asserted the second; op rejects the second. A
sound floor under a fully-designed unified layer is the target. The floor being buildable today is not
permission to stop designing above it.

## The debate to run now

Two obligations, per op: hold more debate on the parts Carmack left unbudgeted, unspecified, and vague; and
answer the genuine faults he raised. The debate is design-forward and adversarial in the way that produces a
thorough design, not a keep-or-cut vote. The specific charge to every participant: keep the maximal shape;
design through the fault; where a piece genuinely cannot be built as described under the no_alloc + dual-locus +
no-JIT-permission constraints, prove that concretely with the exact blocking constraint, and design the maximal
thing that does hold, never a smaller architecture chosen for being easier.

The clusters, hardest first:

- Cluster A: the no_alloc dual-locus relational-engine generator (problem 1) and load-verify coherence
  (problem 2). This is the keystone; the unification stands or falls on whether this engine can be designed in
  full.
- Cluster B: the native tier's platform-capability model and stencil toolchain (problems 3, 4) and the
  interpreter hot loop (problem 5), which together are the runtime execution design the arc has not written.
- Cluster C: an adversarial keeper-honest pass over whatever A and B still hand-wave, plus the provenance and
  dual-locus-authoring and wire-format problems (6, 7, 8) that thread through both, plus a direct attack on the
  YAGNI-demotion framing to confirm the maximal shape is defensible where it is and to force real design where
  it is not.

Per the sequential-cumulative panel discipline, the clusters run in order: B reads A, C reads A and B, so the
runtime design is built on the settled engine design and the honest-keeper pass sees the whole. The output of
each lands as a research doc; this topic is the agenda they answer. Nothing is built until all three land and
the maximal design is whole.
