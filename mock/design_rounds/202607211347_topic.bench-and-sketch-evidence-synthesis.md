## What this topic is, and why it exists now

The arc reached a converged architecture on paper (two-artifact model, graded co-modal proof spine, reachability
lease as a coeffect, the relational-fixpoint compile substrate, the CFG-of-blocks interpreter, copy-and-patch as
a non-primary native tier, incremental scaling as a framework mechanism). Two experiments were named as gating the
bridge commitment and both were marked "later, design-on-paper is the current mode." That order got overtaken.
The de-risking inventory ran to completion ahead of schedule and then well past its floor, covering not just the
two gating questions but most of the load-bearing feasibility and cost questions the design rests on. The result
is a body of evidence that now sits under the converged shape and should be folded into it, because several
findings do not merely confirm a leaning, they change a settled-ish wire-format number, refute a dispatch
assumption the runtime design imported from Deegen, and put concrete sizes on the native-tier question that op's
recenter answered by taste.

The point of writing it down as a topic, rather than leaving it as research artifacts, is that the design proper
should cite numbers where it currently cites intuition, and a reader six months out should find one place that
says "this choice is bench-backed, here is the measurement and here is its caveat." It is also the input to the
next step: an independent audit of both the conclusions and the measurement method, because a bench that measures
the wrong thing is worse than no bench, and several of these findings are load-bearing enough to deserve that
scrutiny before they harden into the design.

The findings split four ways. Some force a change to a decision that was settled or provisionally leaning. Some
prove a feasibility that was assumed or hoped. Some confirm a direction and put a number on it without changing
it. And some do not answer what they were meant to, honestly, and are recorded as such so no one mistakes a
proxy or a swamped measurement for a proof. The toolchain throughout was Zig 0.16.0 and Rust nightly-2026-05-28
on aarch64 Apple Silicon; the branch and interpreter benches ran through the mockspace bench harness (cdylib
variant isolation, cross-validated byte-exact output, hardware-counter timing), the feasibility questions ran as
standalone sketches with WORKS/FAILS outcomes.

## The findings that change a decision

These are the ones that matter most, because each contradicts something the design currently assumes or leans
toward. Each is a place the design text must be corrected, not just annotated.

### The node record is 24 bytes with three inline operands, not 16 with two

Cluster B settled the wire format on "a 16-byte constant-stride record with two inline operands" and carried
arity-at-most-two as a stated assumption with a flag to revisit. The measurement flips it. Across every call
fraction, the 24-byte record (three inline operands) beats the 16-byte record (BN2: 3.82 to 4.28 ns versus 4.43
to 5.65 ns), because inlining the third operand avoids a pool spill on the hot binop path and the sequential
arena stream hides the density penalty of the wider record. The corpus distribution confirms three operands is
the right cut and not one more: arity at most three covers 96.5% of nodes in the Python-stdlib proxy, and a
32-byte four-operand variant ties the 24-byte one exactly (the fourth operand is unused, arity at least four is
1.3%). So 24 bytes is precisely optimal: 16 is too narrow (pays pool indirection on the common case), 32 is
wasted width. The value-arena throughput bench corroborates the mechanism directly: pool indirection is the
expensive part (10.7 ns/node with indirection versus 4.4 ns/node with inline operands). This is a wire-format
decision and wire format is day-one, so this correction lands before any runtime code.

### Interpreter dispatch is a plain switch, and it beats tail-threading for vehje

Cluster B imported Deegen's dispatch shape (Zig `@call(.always_tail)` tail-call threading, "Deegen beats LuaJIT
hand-asm by 31%"), and the recenter softened `preserve_none` to an optimisation-tier concern. The measurement
goes further than the softening and contradicts the import: a plain `switch` is the fastest dispatch across every
case (BN1: switch 4.58, direct-threaded 6.24, tail 6.38 ns/op; the expansion sweep confirms switch faster at
every op-body weight W=1 to 64). The reason is specific to vehje and does not generalise from the dynamic-language
literature: vehje's Core is a lean, statically-checked, flat IR with a bounded op vocabulary (about 25 primitives,
SP1), so the switch's branch is well-predicted and the tail-threading machinery costs more than it saves. The one
regime where tail-threading wins in the literature is a hundred-plus op-kind interpreter, which SP1 shows vehje is
not. Consequence: `preserve_none` leaves the critical path entirely (SK3 already confirmed 0.16 has no
`preserve_none` and it does not matter), and the runtime design should name plain switch as the dispatch, with
tail-threading demoted to a measured-and-rejected alternative for this op vocabulary rather than the default.

### Native is a ceiling of about 1.0x to 1.2x over a good interpreter, not a 10x keystone

The native-tier question was answered by op's recenter through taste (keep the native endgame, do not make it
primary), against Carmack's push to demote it to a research track and against the earlier framing that made
copy-and-patch the keystone. The measurement sizes it and vindicates the recenter's middle position with numbers.
On the same program, fairly measured (L1-resident, non-vectorisable data-dependent index, native arithmetic as an
opaque scalar `madd` so LLVM cannot auto-vectorise the native side to nothing), copy-and-patch native buys only
about 1.0x to 1.2x over a good register interpreter across three regimes (DRAM-gather 1.0x, serial-dependency
1.2x, independent-compute 1.2x). The interpreter's dispatch is already so small (switch plus NaN-box plus
CFG-of-blocks) that an out-of-order core hides it. The one regime where native wins big needs LLVM
auto-vectorisation, which copy-and-patch cannot do; the branch-tier bench shows the scalar copy-and-patch stencil
actually loses to compiled LLVM native at scale (1.47x at n=4096, 2.58x at 16384) because LLVM if-converts and
vectorises where the stencil emits a real mispredicting branch. So the honest sizing is: build the interpreter
floor first because it is near-native for scalar code, keep copy-and-patch as a modest opt-in accelerator (not a
dependency, not the keystone), and reserve the transformative ten-times-class win for a vectorising JIT
(LLVM/cranelift, the environment-plumbing endgame) aimed at the rare genuinely compute-bound consumer. This is
exactly the recenter's "right step, keep the endgame but do not privilege it," now with a measured multiplier
instead of a lean.

### The branch strategy is tier-dependent, so one global choice is wrong in one tier

The primary branch study (the arc op directed explicitly) establishes that the optimal branch-lowering strategy
is not a fixed property of the branch; it depends on the tier the branch runs in. Under interpretation, evaluating
all arms is the worst strategy everywhere (it multiplies the expensive per-node arm-walk: up to 4.0x the field on
the whole-program bench), and every one-arm dispatch strategy (if-chain, jump-table, binary-tree, hot-first) is a
statistical dead heat, so the interpreter picks the simplest (jump-table) and never eval-all. Under native, the
ranking reverses in the regime that suits it: for a cheap-arm many-way unpredictable branch, eval-all (branchless,
compute all arms and select) dominates by 26% over the jump-table baseline, because it dodges the mispredict the
dispatch strategies pay and computing several cheap arms is cheaper than one mispredicting branch; for heavy arms
or few-way branches the one-arm strategies tie and eval-all loses again. The full two-way and multiway maps
(predictability by arm-cost by arity by key-skew) are in `BRANCH_STRATEGY_MAP.md`; the load-bearing result is the
tier reversal. This connects directly to the recenter's new "proof-directed strategy selection" idea (the grade
per region selects the evaluator): the branch map is the measured content of that portfolio, and it sharpens the
copy-and-patch guidance, the stencil emitter must apply this map (emit branchless eval-all for cheap wide
unpredictable branches, a jump-table otherwise), not a fixed shape, which is why the earlier "copy-and-patch loses
at scale" result is a strategy bug, not a tier verdict.

### The comptime kernel must be iterative, it is not a style choice

SK5 settles that the certified-generation comptime kernel and any comptime traversal cannot be recursive:
recursive comptime SIGSEGVs the Zig compiler at about 2500 depth, and it segfaults rather than erroring
gracefully, so it is a hard wall, not a tunable. The iterative defunctionalised work-stack folds 100k nodes clean.
This is already the design's intent (the defunctionalization owed-piece, Reynolds/Danvy), but the measurement
promotes it from "the principled route" to "the only route," and the same discipline binds the load verifier and
the e-graph extractor (SP6: recursive e-graph extraction hangs on the cycles congruence closure creates; must be
an iterative cost fixpoint).

The capstone is the strongest single validation that the pieces compose, not just work in isolation: a
50,000-node script through the full pipeline (lease inference, cheap lowering, interpretation, value-arena
emission, typed validate) runs end to end in 3.4 ms, with the compile stage (3.24 ms) dominating and the runtime
(127 us) cheap, which matches the authoring/templating majority's compile-heavy trivial-execute profile exactly.
Each stage's per-node cost is consistent with its standalone bench, so the pieces compose with predictable
additive costs.

## The directions confirmed and sized (no change, now with a number)

These the design already committed to; the benches put a number on them and, in doing so, retire the risk that
they were unaffordable.

The relational-fixpoint compile substrate is feasible no-alloc and its feared cost is tamed. The semi-naive reach
fixpoint runs 8M nodes and 16M edges in about 50 ms (630 M-edge-OR/s), well within a compile budget, and
whole-column OR beats delta semi-naive for the realistic shallow graphs (two rounds, delta bookkeeping not
repaid), so the lease fixpoint uses whole-column. The e-graph explosion the eqsat lowering stage risked is tamed:
a proper e-graph saturated to fixpoint with commutativity and distributivity grows a consistent about 5.2x linear
(not exponential) in four rounds regardless of depth, because hashcons congruence deduplicates the equivalent
forms, which validates eqsat as dev-time-only and the bounded streaming window as a hard termination-and-memory
safety mechanism (not just tuning) for pathological consumer rule sets. The effect proof, the load-bearing
correctness core, is negligible: thermometer-encoding the grade makes the lattice join a bitwise OR, the graded
bind the same OR, and the inclusion check a subset test, so effect inference runs at 6.14 ns/node and the
inclusion gate at 0.39 ns/node (an 8M program's effect proof discharges in about 3 ms), sharing the exact
bitmask-propagation machinery as the reachability lease fixpoint (one bottom-up DAG join, differing only in
payload).

The incremental scaling layer (D6), which op adopted specifically for the RimWorld/Clausewitz load-bloat concern,
is decisively validated on both axes. A 2000-mod stack cold-loads in 4 ms and warm-loads (edit one mod) in
0.003 ms (one recompile plus 1999 cache hits), so load cost scales with the change, not the stack size. Cold load
parallelises near-linearly with cores (independent mods 5.6x at 8 threads; the realistic override-and-extend
dependency DAG is wide and shallow, so it hits 7.7x, better than independent because wide levels keep cores
busy), with a serial interner-merge tail of 6.2 ms (a pure shardable dedupe, the next lever if needed). Warm
incremental recompile is bimodal and statically knowable: about 1% of the stack for a typical edit, up to 40% for
a foundational base-mod edit, driven by the transitive-dependent count. The content-addressed caching plus
differential-dataflow recompute answers the concern that motivated D6.

Every Core form's lowering is sized, which turns the lowering rules from asserted to measured. Match lowers to a
jump-table for dense keys (flat 0.51 ns, wins at four-plus arms), a hot-first if-chain for a profiled hot arm, an
if-chain for few sparse arms, a binary tree for many sparse arms; the jump-table's flat 0.51 ns confirms
tag-dispatch is essentially free, which validates the cheap branching on the kind-tagged NaN-box value model.
Project (field access) resolves to a direct offset where the shape is static (0.54 ns, the majority under
ahead-of-time compilation) and a self-disabling monomorphic inline cache where dynamic, with the load-bearing
hazard that an inline cache is the worst strategy at a megamorphic site (1.89 ns) so it must detect that and fall
to a hash lookup; the record's shape_id is the one discriminant serving value branching, match dispatch, and
field caching. Iter fusion is mandatory (materialized intermediates need allocation, forbidden), pull-based
(strictly more composable, supports take/first), with the fusion edge growing with pipeline depth. Resolve uses a
flat shadow-stack symbol table (0.91 ns/ref, O(1), 6x to 14x faster than the O(depth) scope-chain walks).
Interpolation output-building ships the data-driven span-list default (about 1 GB/s, never a bottleneck) with the
reserve/commit sink exposing the cursor for in-place formatting (1.5x). Partial evaluation folds block-structured
templates 3.8x to 7.0x (each static block collapses to one constant), the "compile the template, run only the
holes" property made quantitative. The interpreter value representation is NaN-boxing (8B, 18% over tagged-8B,
stores floats natively where tagged cannot), and the interpreter is a register VM (1.86x over a stack VM, half the
instructions at near-equal per-instruction cost). Record-update reuse (exact-meet in-place) is a pure win on both
axes: frontier-bounded peak memory (SK16) and 1.5x to 9.7x faster mutation, the entire cost paid at emit time.

Two vindications land as by-products. The dispatch-heavy expansion confirms switch beats tail at every op-body
weight, and the arena-locality sweep shows interpreter ns/node is flat for backward windows from 2 to 1M and only
+33% at full-4M random, so the cache cliff is soft and far out and the backward-local discipline is a modest,
non-fragile benefit rather than a fragile requirement.

## What the numbers did not settle (recorded honestly, not as proof)

Several benches did not reproduce their prior finding, or measured a proxy, or were swamped. They are recorded so
no one cites them as confirmation.

The cheap-lowering bench (`hx_cheaplower`) does not show a time win: the benefit of const-fold plus CSE is
node-count reduction (76% fewer nodes), not per-op time, because LLVM already CSEs the recompute, so the
hash-cons lookup is pure overhead in a time bench. The node-count reduction is the real and design-relevant
result; the time framing was the wrong lens. The output-building port (`hx_output`) is within noise at harness
sizes; the original 1.5x needs longer values and higher volume than the per-call byte budget exercises (the
in-harness `interp-output-building` bench, run at proper sizes, does show the 1.5x). The micro-op transfer-function
races (`hx_effect`, `hx_tnum`) are between operations so cheap that which is "baseline" is arbitrary; the design
findings they support (thermometer encoding makes the lattice a flat OR; tnum linear ops are about 1 ns) stand
independently of the race. The tnum multiply is the one genuinely expensive numeric op (149 ns, a 64-bit
long-multiplication loop) and needs a known-operand fast path (both-known lowers to a 0.68 ns concrete multiply,
covering multiply-by-constant), with only unknown-times-unknown falling to the loop, paid demand-driven.

Two whole categories were dropped as not harness-shaped rather than measured. Cache-at-scale effects
(arena-locality beyond L1, large-table gather) need buffers far beyond the 16384-byte harness cap and would only
measure noise at these sizes, so they live as standalone measurements, not harness benches. Binary-size and
compile-time-cliff measurements are size and time-at-compile, not runtime timing.

The corpus distributions (SP2: arity, depth, live-binder-width, closure-rate) that back the 24-byte record and
the u64 live-binder bitmask are measured on a Python-stdlib proxy, not on the real vehje consumers, which do not
exist yet. The distributions are load-bearing (they justify the wire format), so a census-specific corpus
measurement is owed when the real authoring/templating consumers exist; until then the proxy is a reasonable but
unproven stand-in and is named as such.

The copy-and-patch native tier is proven feasible at the mechanism level (the MAP_JIT probe) but the
stencil-extraction toolchain (the object-format work that turns a semantic definition into shippable stencils) is
still deferred (weeks of work), so the per-branch strategy ranking under a full archetype-JIT is taken to follow
the native tier's, which it approximates, rather than measured directly.

## Measurement discipline and its limits (the audit surface)

Because several of these findings are load-bearing, the method matters as much as the result, and the discipline
used is stated here so it can be checked. The branch and interpreter benches ran through the mockspace bench
harness, which compiles each strategy as its own cdylib and loads it in subprocess isolation, so no cross-variant
LTO or inlining can blur a comparison, and every strategy in a bench is cross-validated to produce byte-identical
output (a strategy that computes a different answer is rejected, not timed). The known LLVM defeats were handled
explicitly: branch arms are `#[inline(never)]` so a real branch stays a real branch (LLVM if-converts a cheap
branch to branchless csel otherwise, which would erase the very mispredict the branch bench measures); the copy
elision that made `hx_reuse` show copy equal to reuse was defeated with `core::hint::black_box` so the memcpy is
real; the auto-vectorisation that made the naive native baseline collapse to near-zero was defeated by an opaque
scalar `madd` on a data-dependent index, which is the whole reason the native-ceiling number is 1.2x and not a
misleading 10x. The interp benches are normalised against a baseline strategy (the mockspace `[bench.*.normalise]`
declarative baseline subtraction) so the per-node interpreter dispatch overhead does not swamp the strategy delta,
which is what made the early "strategies tie under interp" read wrong until the baseline was subtracted.

The honest limits of the method: the harness caps working sets at 16384 bytes, so anything cache-bound past L1 is
out of scope (hence the dropped cache-at-scale category); the interp tier is a real tree-walking or CFG
interpreter over a mockup IR, not the eventual vehje runtime, so its absolute numbers are representative-not-final
and the strategy rankings are the transferable part; and the branch archetypes are intentionally-shaped synthetic
arm bodies (cheap W=1 versus heavy W=24, linear versus nested versus blocks) chosen to find each strategy's sweet
spot, not sampled from real consumer code, so they map the strategy space rather than predict a specific
consumer's mix. The audit that follows this topic should check exactly these: that each bench measures the thing
its conclusion claims (not a tautology or an artifact), that the LLVM-defeat measures are actually defeating what
they claim, and that the interp-tier and proxy-corpus results are read as directional where they are directional.

## What still needs building (named, not feasibility gaps)

These are construction items, not open questions. The feasibility is proven; the full mechanism is not built.

The full multi-relation join engine (leapfrog-triejoin, AGM bounds) and the eqsat query over the relational
substrate (SP5 scoped to the reach query, SP6 to bounded saturate-then-extract). The slotted/colored/graded
e-graph extensions and the streaming window over the bounded base. DBSP-style counted differential dataflow for
retraction (BN3's additive edit case works; general edit needs it). The generated-continuation and prefix-sharing
DFS for the `Handle` form (SK2 proved the bounded-enumeration core; the general resumable-handler codegen is
owed). The `lambda_veh` proof document (T1/T2/T3), a writing artifact. The census-specific corpus measurement when
real consumers exist. The native copy-and-patch stencil-extraction toolchain and its versus-interpreter bench,
which the numbers suggest is regime-specific anyway and therefore correctly non-primary.

## Implications for the design proper

The corrections that should land in the design text, in priority order. First, the wire format: change the record
to 24 bytes with three inline operands and drop the arity-at-most-two assumption (this is day-one and blocks
runtime code). Second, the runtime dispatch: name plain switch as the interpreter dispatch and demote tail-call
threading to a measured-and-rejected alternative for vehje's op vocabulary, and drop `preserve_none` from the
critical path entirely. Third, the native tier: record the measured 1.0x-to-1.2x ceiling and frame copy-and-patch
as an opt-in accelerator whose stencil emitter applies the tier-dependent branch map, with the vectorising JIT as
the only path to the ten-times class, which is the recenter's position now backed by numbers. Fourth, the comptime
kernel and the load verifier and the e-graph extractor are all stated as iterative work-stacks, not recursive,
because recursion is a hard compiler wall not a style choice. Fifth, the branch-strategy map becomes the content
of the recenter's proof-directed strategy-selection portfolio, cited where the design says "the grade per region
selects the evaluator."

The confirmations do not require design changes but should be cited: the relational substrate cost (50 ms at 8M),
the eqsat linear-5x growth bound, the effect thermometer cost (about 3 ms at 8M), the incremental scaling numbers
(4 ms cold, 0.003 ms warm, 7.7x parallel) as the concrete answer to op's load-bloat concern, and the capstone
3.4 ms end-to-end as the evidence the pieces compose. The honest non-results (cheap-lowering is node-count not
time, the proxy corpus, the deferred stencil toolchain) should be named where the relevant decisions cite their
support, so a future reader does not over-read a proxy or a swamped bench.
